The boards are identical, so the feed level gap is real; and a basis error of my own

Three results, and the first is the one that matters.

THE BOARDS AGREE EXACTLY. board_scale_test on 40 pin routes, k held at 1.0 in both calls
and only the boards object differing: median 1.000, p25 1.000, p75 1.000, min 1.000, max
1.000. Not one route moved. Hub departure board after dedupe, same week both readers, cache
median 458 against store median 458. So CacheBoards and OagBoards return the same qshare, k
is directly comparable across the two paths, and the back-test ran the connecting feed at
one seventeenth of the shipped level. That was the worse of the two branches and it is the
one that landed.

THE CONSEQUENCE IS ARITHMETIC, not a new run. k is a pure multiplier, so LEVEL-VS-SHAPE's
measured median re-levels directly: the QSI feed at k=0.06 needed x1.62 to reach actual
connecting on the 1,881 routes intersecting the two arms, so at k=1.0 it would over-read
that quantity by circa 10.3 times on the median route. Against that, FEED-LEVELS has the
live path at k=1.0 reading 24,080 two-way against the 2025 analyst's 25,999, which is 0.93x.
Both are real measurements and they cannot both describe one quantity.

Stating the basis of each before anyone reaches for the comparison. The back-test's actual
connecting is outturn_pax less p2p_outturn, a Sabre-derived residual on routes that
launched. The analyst's is his own connecting forecast on one route that has not.
FLOOR-EVIDENCED measured Sabre under-counting transfer traffic at 2.07x on 335 routes
touching a major Asian hub and 2.50x on 476 long-haul routes, and SJC-TPE is both. So part
of a ten-fold gap is a known under-count in the grading quantity and the rest is not
accounted for. This is not resolved and must not be resolved by argument.

Distinct departing flights in load_legs' own 15th-to-21st window:

  2017-08   JNB 24 to 396    CAI 47 to 277    DXB 379 to 632    DOH 216 to 301
  2016-03   JNB 23 to 431    CAI 43 to 244    DXB 384 to 672    DOH 178 to 356

The control held. TPE 403 monthly, 1 in the half-year, 403 together. LHR 763 monthly, 78 in
the half-year, 763 together, so the London flights that appear in both are absorbed by
distinct counting rather than double counted. JNB recovers to 396 against 401 in 2018-08 and
DXB to 632 against 639, so the recovered world is the right size.

A BASIS ERROR OF MINE, in the tool written this morning to catch basis errors. The
--coverage table counts distinct flights in the whole label with no date window, so a
half-year label counts six months of schedule variation while a month counts one month.
Scoring every label against a single overall best held months against half-years: JNB's
"best 528" was 2015-H2, which flagged most of 2018 to 2025 as SHORT when those months are
complete. Within grain, JNB's monthly best is 473 and 2018-08 reads 85% rather than 76%.
Grains are now scored separately and never against each other. The 2015-2017 monthly labels
still read 4 to 5% and that finding is untouched.
