Three grading targets, so the claim set can be re-measured under each basis

FLAG BEFORE ANY RUN, because "what happens to the 92 and 86" presupposes knowing what they
are. Those two figures appear in exactly three places, all 12 August documents, and in no
code: the handover, GRADING-BASIS-QUESTION and the GRADE-BASIS log entry. bt2_claimset's own
docstring says the published fitted figures are 89.8% within +-20% and 82.5% within +-10% on
n=2,915 over 2016-2019 and 2025, and that its reconstruction returns 89.2% and 81.4%.
ESTATE-INDEX version 11 says 89% and 82% on 2,915 launches. So 92 and 86 are either a
different sample, a different config, or a figure that propagated without a source.

The run sidesteps it by reporting the FULL claim set under each target, so whatever the
published pair turns out to be, it is in the table. And there is no single "the claim":
bt2_claimset produces the calibrated figure at +-20% and +-10%, the blind route-level figure
which is the circa 60%, tier A on the narrowest tenth of predicted spread, and portfolios of
10 and 20 which is the published 94%. A basis change moves five numbers and they will not
move together.

THE THREE TARGETS, all measured on the same pair in the same launch year so only the
definition moves:

  nonstop      launch_pax, itinerary='NON-STOP'. THE DEFAULT, and what every published
               figure describes. An existing run is unchanged.
  p2p_outturn  backtest.py's local definition, screening the connecting column rather than
               the itinerary label. A target in its own right because it ought to equal
               launch_pax and nobody has checked, while the pin grades fc_over_p2p against
               one and the model trains on the other.
  sector       the leg-adjacency total, the whole quantity a client is shown.

A missing alt_targets file is a hard stop rather than a fall back to the default, because a
run that silently graded on launch_pax while the operator believed it was grading on sector
would produce the published figure under a heading saying otherwise. load_clean prints the
target beside the artifact count, since the 1.1x-seats rule catches a different number of
routes under each target and two targets therefore produce two populations.

THE CONTROL RUNS FIRST. launch_pax is recomputed with bt2_discover's own filter and compared
against what discovery already wrote. More than one pair in a hundred disagreeing stops the
run, because then the filter reproduced here is not the filter discovery used and neither
new target can be trusted.

All three queries were tested against a hand-built table with known answers before touching
the store: five itineraries on one pair including a reverse-direction nonstop and an
SJC-LAX-TPE where the two airports are not consecutive. nonstop returns 107 and p2p returns
107, both summing the reverse direction; sector returns 172 and puts the LAX itinerary's 10
passengers onto SJC-LAX and LAX-TPE instead.
