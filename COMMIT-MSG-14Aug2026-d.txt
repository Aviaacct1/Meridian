Show what the curfew costs, and the seat basis, on the page

optimise_departure has always computed the unrestricted optimum beside the
permitted one and returned unrestricted_dep, unrestricted_score and cost_pax with
it, expressly so the cost of a restriction can be quoted rather than hidden. Its
own docstring calls that figure the argument an airport takes to a curfew review
and says it is worth more than the forecast it comes from. cortex_app puts the
whole dict into the payload at schedule.optimised. The page read res.optimised,
which is the airline and gauge block, and never touched schedule.optimised, so
neither the unrestricted departure nor its cost has ever reached a screen.

The schedule panel now shows the restriction entered, the permitted departure,
the departure the route would take without the restriction, and the cost. Hidden
unless a restriction was entered, because with no curfew there is no cost.

The basis is printed under the figure, because a reader will assume the wrong
one. cost_pax is the difference in connecting DEMAND won at the two departure
times, scored with no capture scaling, no connectivity re-split and no capacity
cap between it and the client. It is not a carried-passenger figure. Where the
run is capacity bound the seats sell at either departure time and only the mix
moves, so the carried cost is smaller and can be nil; the page says so whenever
the run spills.

Also puts the seat count and seats_source on the schedule line. The payload has
carried both since the carrier configuration was measured on 10 August and the
page showed neither, so a schedule sized on China Airlines' own 306-seat A350-900
read on screen exactly like one sized on the generic 336. That is how the
fixed-gauge defect corrected this morning ran unnoticed: a basis that is in the
payload and not on the page is a basis nobody checks.

Verified by parse and by a behaviour check over three payload shapes: no curfew
entered, curfew without spill, curfew with spill.
