Curve PNG: add the uncapped total demand line (John Carter, 24 Aug 2026)

John, once the picture was finally landing: the two-line chart (connecting demand,
route total carried) hides how much demand a poor departure time actually spills,
because the total line is capped at the aircraft ceiling (87.5% plan load factor in
this run) while P2P itself is never capped anywhere in the engine - only the route
total is. Asked whether a third line, the uncapped total, was easy to add.

It was: _curve_series() already computed p2p_carried + connecting_ew before applying
the capacity cap, then discarded it. Now stored on each point as demand_ew alongside
the existing total_ew (still the capped figure everything else reads). render_curve_png()
plots it as a third, dashed amber line under the two solid lines, so wherever demand
sits at or under the ceiling the lines read exactly as before, and wherever a departure
would spill demand the gap between the dashed line and the solid navy total line shows
it directly.

Y-axis headroom calc extended to include the new line (it can now be the chart's own
highest point). No change to the callout, the insight caption or any other reader of
total_ew - those all still read the capped figure, which is what is actually carried
and what the headline numbers state.

Verified: 34/34 regression checks still pass (no test touches the new field, so this
needed a visual check, not just the suite) - rendered against a reconstructed version
of today's live SJC-TPE/CI/B789/5x curve and confirmed by eye: the amber line clearly
separates from the navy line in the small hours and around midnight (where uncapped
demand runs to circa 250,000 two-way against a circa 131,000 ceiling) and rejoins it
through the middle of the day and at the chosen 20:59 departure, where the route is at
or near the ceiling regardless.
