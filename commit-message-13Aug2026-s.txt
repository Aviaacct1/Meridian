Isolate the year-round services, because annualising the seats was not enough

The correction moved things the right way and did not finish the job. seats x k on the
sector total went from 21.1% to 35.5% and the local leg from 16.3% to 19.3%, against a null
control figure of 74.5% on a matched basis.

THE RESIDUAL HAS A NAMED CAUSE. Median months operated is 6 and p10 is 2, so scaling by
12/months assumes a route that flew six months in its launch year goes on to fly twelve. On a
genuinely seasonal service those months are the operating pattern rather than a partial view
of it, annualising overstates the seats, and the ratio is dragged down. The arithmetic
agrees: k for the local leg reads 0.347 here against the null control's 0.673, a ratio of
1.94 against the x2.0 the median months implies.

--min-months isolates it. 11 selects year-round services, where the scaling is nearly a
no-op. If seats x k on the sector total then approaches 74.5%, seasonality was the whole
residual and the test is usable on that subset alone.

THE JOIN IS THE REAL LIMIT AND IT CAPS THIS LINE OF WORK. 1,479 of the arm's 3,072 rows have
no launch_profile match at all, so nearly half the population is unreachable before any
filter, and the long-haul cut leaves 730. The arm forecasts and grades at L+1 while
launch_profile describes L, and nothing available says whether a route ran year-round in the
graded year: the arm's own service column knows, but LF-MEASUREMENT-VOID established that its
capacity column is annualised at 52 weeks and unusable.

The clean version is an arm at --offset 0, which grades against the launch year and puts
seats, traffic and forecast on one set of months with no annualising at all. Forty-five
minutes, and it removes the entire class of error this script has now hit twice.

THE FIGURE THAT HOLDS ACROSS BOTH RUNS, because it has no seats in it: on 730 long-haul
international FSC routes the engine's captured_uncapped reads a median 0.617 of actual local
outturn and lands within +-20% on 13.4% of them. The clamp and the switch cannot repair
that. The synthetic check established they only touch routes wrong in the tail, and 0.617 is
a fault in the middle. So the long-haul local leg wants a level correction measured directly,
not a reconciliation against the aircraft, and that is a smaller and better-posed piece of
work than the cross-check.
