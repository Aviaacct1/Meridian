Market background panel: measured pre-run market brief on the dashboard

From Jarek Zych's tester feedback (24 Aug 2026); form agreed on
MOCKUP-market-background-24Aug2026.html before build. Once origin and
destination resolve, a collapsed strip renders the measured market facts
(P2P market, direct service today, leading carrier, catchment population,
connecting potential) and expands to charts: demand series by year, carrier
share, how the market travels today, cabin mix, connecting potential over
each end, the deduped nonstop service table, catchment summary. SVG/CSV
downloads per cell.

Design rules carried: measured fact only, NO forecast content, so the panel
can never disagree with a run; flag-rather-than-fill (a store that cannot
answer a section says so; the carrier cell introspects the Sabre schema for
a carrier column rather than assuming one); OAG dedupe via the wave_cache
distinct-flight rule with days-of-op masks unioned; server cache per
(origin, dest, week, year); the request fires in the background and never
blocks Run; collapsed by default, state remembered.

Also in this batch, from the same feedback: the panel collapses to its
strip when a run starts (an expanded panel pushed the results below the
fold; contextual, the user's saved open/closed preference is not written),
New forecast clears and hides the panel, and curfewSanity catches the
inverted-curfew entry Jarek actually hit live: he entered 05:30-23:00
meaning operating hours, the tool lawfully picked 04:59, and nothing said
why. A restriction of 14 hours or more now stops the run with the swap
spelled out - an error, not a silent auto-swap, because the tool must not
rewrite a fact on the user's behalf. Wired on both Run and Optimise.

Return arrival and turnaround (John's ruling, 24 Aug): "illustrative" was a
development label; near launch a number rests on a stated decision. The
schedule line now states the return's basis in words (type-standard
turnaround / caller-set turnaround / caller-set arrival) instead of
"indicative". New inputs: Return arrival (origin local, quarter-hours,
blank = turnaround-based) derives the return departure to hit the caller's
time exactly; Turnaround (min, blank = type/sector standard) replaces the
number not the basis and rides into the departure screen, the schedule draw
and the optimiser cache key (a cached optimum taken under the standard turn
must not answer for the caller's). A rotation that cannot physically turn
at the destination inside the stated turnaround is flagged on the schedule
line, never silently moved; curfew flags append rather than replace. The
curfew screen on the return now tests the ACTUAL arrival (caller's time
when set) - the old derivation assumed the default draw. Return timing
moves no demand anywhere in the engine (both feed sides price off the
outbound departure), so all of this is schedule drawing, not forecasting.
Deliberately NOT built: optimising the return on traffic - it would strand
the aircraft, and there is no demand signal for it to optimise.

Tail rotation chart (John, 24 Aug; rebuilt week-native same day on his
ruling that day-of-week allocation lands soon and the chart must not need
a second build then): in the results under the schedule line, how many
aircraft the route ties up and why, drawn over one week. Arithmetic on
block and the stated turnaround. THE SEAM FOR DAY-OF-WEEK IS tailPattern()
AND NOTHING ELSE: count (tailCountCirc, circular so a Day-7 rotation
spilling past the week counts against Day 1), row assignment and drawing
all take an explicit list of departure day-offsets plus a stated basis.
Today that is daily (fact) or consecutive days (labelled working
assumption, the pattern named as the airline's choice); when the engine
allocates real days it sends schedule.operating_days and the dormant
branch takes over with no other change. Verified: 10h daily -> 1 tail,
12h daily -> 2, 12h 5x consecutive -> 2, 12h Mon/Wed/Fri -> 1 (the
day-choice dividend, now visible), week-wrap case -> 2. _schedule_times
returns turn_min and turn_basis so the chart states the same basis as the
schedule line. Answers Jarek's aircraft-required item (9) visually.
