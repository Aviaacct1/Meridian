Turnarounds measured from OAG by aircraft type, replacing three invented numbers

JOHN, 14 August: "we do it by aircraft type average turn, which might be better... calculate
turns from 2025 OAG."

He is right on both counts. The aeroplane sets the stand time and region only proxies for
it, and the 60, 90 and 180 shipped this afternoon were planning averages with no source
sitting in a place that decides which departures are flyable at all.

app/build_turnarounds.py (new)
  THE FILTER IS THE WHOLE METHOD. OAG carries no tail numbers, so an arrival cannot be
  linked with certainty to the departure that reuses the aircraft. At a hub that is fatal:
  an arrival and a departure of the same type ten minutes apart are two different
  aeroplanes and a naive minimum gap reports a ten-minute turn. Only UNAMBIGUOUS
  station-days are measured, where one carrier operated exactly one arrival and exactly one
  departure of one type at one airport on one day. There the gap is that aircraft's turn and
  nothing else, and it is also the case a route forecast cares about, since a new service is
  an outstation operation and that is where the turnaround binds.

  Excluded, each counted and reported rather than dropped in silence: charter and freight
  (service_type J only); through-flight transit stops, which are not station turns; gaps of
  eight hours or more, which are night stops and a real finding in their own right; and gaps
  under twenty minutes, which are not credible pairings.

  Reports n and the 10th, 25th and 50th percentiles per type. THE FIGURE TO USE IS p10, NOT
  THE MEDIAN: a turnaround is the time the aeroplane needs, and the median mixes in stations
  that simply had a long gap.

app/route_feed.py
  turnaround_mins() takes an aircraft_code and prefers the measured p10 where the type has a
  usable sample. The flight-type bands remain as the fallback and are now labelled in the
  docstring as planning averages with no source, which is what they are. A type measured on
  too few station-days is marked unusable in the file and is not read.

app/cortex_app.py
  Passes the aircraft to the resolver, so a run with a named gauge gets that gauge's turn.

Tested on a fixture built to a known answer: an outstation pair lands at exactly 180, a hub
with two arrivals and two departures of one type is skipped as ambiguous, and a ten-hour
overnight is excluded as a night stop rather than counted as a turn. The reader prefers a
usable measured p10 of 145, falls back to 180 where the sample is too small or the type is
absent, and a case override beats both.
