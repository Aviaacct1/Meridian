The QSI connecting feed is measurably worse than the flat feed, tested cleanly, and does not ship

No engine code in this commit. It is the record of a day of measurement, a correction to the
runbook found by running it, and two defects in how the arms are wired that have to be fixed
before the next connecting A/B.

1. THE RESULT

The feed tested with everything else held constant. Both arms carry feed_cfg, so the
point-to-point carrier zeroing, the dominance scaling and the behind rate are on in BOTH and
cancel; the only difference that can move a number is the feed itself.

  FORECAST over TOTAL OUTTURN, n=2,963 paired
      flat feed    median 0.489   within +-20%  15.1%
      QSI feed     median 0.412   within +-20%  12.7%
      +43 -112, p<0.001                          MEASURABLE AND WORSE

  FORECAST over PURE P2P     +0 -0, identical, as it must be: the feed cannot move that column

THE SWITCH DOES NOT SHIP.

Pinned route set, 4,342 routes, launch years 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2024, graded against 2017,
2018, 2019 and 2025. The pin matters: backtest.py line 928 records that discovery membership is
not stable run to run, and compare_backtest_arms pairs on the routes all arms scored.

2. k COULD NOT HAVE RESCUED IT

The obvious objection is that the feed ran at an uncalibrated k of 0.06. Actual connecting per
route is outturn_pax less p2p_outturn; each feed's error is log(actual / feed), n=1,881:

  flat feed    needs x1.28    IQR of log error 2.666
  QSI feed     needs x1.62    IQR of log error 2.840

k fixes the multiplier and cannot touch the IQR, and the QSI IQR is WIDER. Do not spend time
calibrating k on this evidence.

3. THE ARM WAS CONFOUNDED, AND THE CONFOUND TURNED OUT NOT TO MATTER

The first comparison run was the QSI arm against a BARE control, and it was not a test of the
feed. feed_cfg is None in a bare control, because backtest.py builds it only under --feed-fix,
--mct-banking, --qsi-feed or --no-split-floor, and THREE behaviours in route_feed.py gate on
feed_cfg merely EXISTING rather than on qsi_feed:

  - P2P_CARRIERS zeroing, lines 223 and 362. Twenty-eight low-cost airlines have both sides of
    the feed returned as 0.0, outright.
  - dominance scaling in _cap_eff: base x (0.5 + dominance), so a carrier holding 10% of the
    hub gets 0.6x the feed and only one holding half of it gets 1.0x.
  - behind_cap 0.10 replacing the default capture on the behind side.

Measured separately, control against the same configuration with no QSI feed, n=2,974: median
0.465 to 0.482, within +-20% 14.2% to 15.0%, +98 -74, p=0.079. NOT MEASURABLE, and pointing the
right way. So the bundle is worth about +0.8 points and cannot be told from noise, while the
feed is worth -2.4 and can. The confound was real and immaterial.

It was found through the zeros. The QSI arm returned a zero feed on 944 of 3,072 routes against
the flat feed's 166, and of those 944, 817 carry a P2P_CARRIERS airline while of the 2,128
non-zero routes NOT ONE does. Removing them leaves 2,255 routes the feed actually processed, of
which it zeroed 127, 5.6%, against the flat feed's 5.4%. THERE IS NO SILENT-ZERO DEFECT IN THE
QSI FEED.

4. THE CONNECTIVITY FLOOR CANNOT BE MEASURED BY THIS BACK-TEST

route_forecast line 760 computes carried = min(total_demand, capacity x max_plan_lf) BEFORE the
floor block, and the floor only re-splits carried into P2P and connecting. The code states it at
line 767: "WITHOUT changing the total". forecast_pax is the carried total, so both ratio columns
are identical with the floor on or off, and the third arm would have reported nothing.

TO TEST IT, the row dict must carry the POST-SPLIT p2p and connecting forecast. That is a small
change to backtest.py, not a new experiment. Open item 2 stays open.

And because the floor is invisible, --no-split-floor with no --qsi-feed IS the --feed-fix arm as
far as either ratio column can see. The arm run on the instinct to complete the three turned out
to be the clean control the day needed, and the planned --feed-fix run was cancelled.

5. TWO DEFECTS FOUND BY RUNNING THE RUNBOOK

THE M COUNTER CANNOT FIRE UNDER --jobs. "QSI feed: N without a flown schedule, M in-run
fallbacks" reads feed_cfg in the PARENT, while backtest.py line 455 and route_feed.py lines 273
and 420 increment those counters inside worker processes holding their own pickled copy from
_worker_init. Under --jobs 12 both are structurally 0, and the arm duly reported N=0 where the
coverage check said circa 1,450. The gate set for this run could not have fired. FIX IT OR
DELETE IT: a gate that cannot fire reads as a pass.

The replacement is better than the counter: diff fc_over_out between two arm CSVs. Routes where
the feed fired differ; routes that fell back are identical. 2,460 of 3,072 differ, 80.1%,
matching the independent coverage check.

THE WAVE CACHE MUST BE REBUILT WHENEVER THE PIN CHANGES. The six-year cache covered 16.7% of
this pin, 727 of 4,342, because its flown table holds only the routes it was built from. Rebuilt
against the pin: 66.6% on the pin, 80.1% on routes that produce a gradeable row. The residual
third is SEASONAL and is a limit on what was tested: wave_cache takes each route's flown schedule
from ONE week, the first single-week label of Y+1, and every such label in the store is late May.
Winter and shoulder-season services have no flown schedule, so the feed was tested on summer
operators.

6. THE RULER WAS CHECKED BEFORE ANYTHING WAS WRITTEN DOWN

Everything rests on fc_over_out. ZERO routes of 3,072 have outturn_pax below p2p_outturn, so the
grading quantity is valid. 635 routes, 20.7%, have them exactly equal, which is a route carrying
no connecting traffic at all and is legitimate.

7. THE FINDING WORTH MORE THAN THE ARM RESULT

An IQR of 2.666 in logs is a factor of FOURTEEN across the middle half of routes, and it applies
to BOTH feeds. Neither the flat feed nor the QSI feed carries much information about how much
connecting traffic an individual new route will carry. The argument between them has been an
argument about which of two uninformative estimates to prefer.

The open question is not which feed. It is whether route-level connecting traffic is forecastable
from this data at all, and that belongs in how the connecting number is presented to a client,
not only in the backlog.

8. TWO THINGS ON THE SHIPPED PATH

The flat feed hands a connecting forecast to 567 of the 635 routes that carry none, 89% of them,
while under-reading volume by 28% where traffic does exist. That is where a client's number comes
from today.

P2P_CARRIERS is NOT costing accuracy: the bundle containing it scores slightly better than the
control. Open item 7 stands as an improvement candidate, weight rather than zero, since Ryanair
and Wizz passengers self-connect in volume. It is the largest component of the bundle, 817 of
3,072 routes.

9. WHAT THE RESULT DOES NOT COVER

k was not calibrated. Only summer-operating routes were tested. Both feeds are near-uninformative
at route level. And the engine under-reads total outturn by more than half in every arm, so this
is a comparison between two poor options rather than a search for a good one.

10. THE LESSON THAT SHOULD OUTLIVE THE DAY

A config object that is None in one arm and a dict in the other is not one switch, it is four.
Three behaviours in route_feed key off "if feed_cfg" rather than off the named flag, so any flag
that creates the object turns all of them on. Worth a sweep for the same shape elsewhere before
the next A/B is designed.
