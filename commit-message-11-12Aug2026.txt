The connecting arm compared four changes at once, and the QSI feed remains untested

No engine code in this commit. It is the record of a day of measurement, a correction to the
runbook found by running it, and a finding about how the arms are wired that has to be fixed
before any connecting A/B can mean anything.

1. WHAT WAS RUN

Control (flat feed) against --qsi-feed, both with split_floor ON, on a PINNED set of 4,342
routes, launch years 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2024, graded against 2017, 2018, 2019 and 2025.
The pin matters: backtest.py line 928 records that discovery membership is not stable run to
run, and compare_backtest_arms pairs on the routes all arms scored.

  FORECAST over PURE P2P     identical to three decimals, +0 -0
  FORECAST over TOTAL, n=2,963
      control      median 0.469   within +-20%  14.2%
      --qsi-feed   median 0.412   within +-20%  12.7%
      +68 -113, p=0.001

2. AND THAT NUMBER CANNOT BE ATTRIBUTED TO THE QSI FEED

feed_cfg is None in the control, because backtest.py builds it only under --feed-fix,
--mct-banking, --qsi-feed or --no-split-floor. THREE behaviours in route_feed.py gate on
feed_cfg merely EXISTING rather than on qsi_feed:

  - P2P_CARRIERS zeroing, lines 223 and 362. Twenty-eight low-cost airlines have both sides
    of the feed returned as 0.0, outright.
  - dominance scaling in _cap_eff: base x (0.5 + dominance), so a carrier holding 10% of the
    hub gets 0.6x the feed and only a carrier holding half of it gets 1.0x.
  - behind_cap 0.10 replacing the default capture on the behind side.

So the arm compared FOUR changes and the p=0.001 belongs to none of them individually.

MEASURED, NOT INFERRED. Of the 944 routes where the QSI arm returned a zero feed, 817 carry a
P2P_CARRIERS airline, and of the 2,128 non-zero routes NOT ONE does. The rule is absolute.
Removing those 817 leaves 2,255 routes the feed actually processed, of which it zeroed 127,
5.6%, against the flat feed's 5.4%.

THERE IS NO SILENT-ZERO DEFECT IN THE QSI FEED. The zeros are the carrier list.

3. THE CLEAN TEST, AND IT NEEDS NO NEW CODE

--feed-fix builds feed_cfg with the same behind_cap, dom_gain and dom_floor and leaves
qsi_feed off. So --feed-fix against --qsi-feed differs in the feed alone, and one 45-minute
run produces the comparison this day was supposed to produce.

Arms 2 and 3 are ALREADY a clean pair for a different question: both carry feed_cfg with
identical settings and differ only in split_floor, so arm 2 against arm 3 isolates the
connectivity floor. That is the one single-variable comparison the day produced.

THE ARM THIS MESSAGE'S EARLIER DRAFT RECOMMENDED, the control with --no-split-floor, would NOT
have worked: --no-split-floor itself builds feed_cfg and brings the carrier zeroing and the
dominance scaling with it. Another four-variable arm dressed as a one-variable one.

4. TWO DEFECTS FOUND BY RUNNING THE RUNBOOK

THE M COUNTER CANNOT FIRE UNDER --jobs. "QSI feed: N without a flown schedule, M in-run
fallbacks" reads feed_cfg in the PARENT, while backtest.py line 455 and route_feed.py lines
273 and 420 increment those counters inside worker processes holding their own pickled copy
from _worker_init. Under --jobs 12 both are structurally 0, and the arm duly reported N=0
where the coverage check said circa 1,450. The gate set for this run could not have fired.

The replacement is better than the counter: diff fc_over_out between the two arm CSVs. Routes
where the feed fired differ; routes that fell back are identical. 2,460 of 3,072 differ,
80.1%, matching the independent coverage check.

THE WAVE CACHE MUST BE REBUILT WHENEVER THE PIN CHANGES. The six-year cache covered 16.7% of
this pin, 727 of 4,342, because its flown table holds only the routes it was built from.
Rebuilt against the pin: 66.6% on the pin, 80.1% on routes that produce a gradeable row. The
residual third is SEASONAL: wave_cache takes each route's flown schedule from ONE week, the
first single-week label of Y+1, and every such label in the store is late May. Winter and
shoulder-season services have no flown schedule, so the feed is tested on summer operators.

5. THE RULER WAS CHECKED BEFORE ANYTHING WAS WRITTEN DOWN

Everything rests on fc_over_out. ZERO routes of 3,072 have outturn_pax below p2p_outturn, so
the grading quantity is valid. 635 routes, 20.7%, have them exactly equal, which is a route
carrying no connecting traffic at all and is legitimate.

6. THE FINDING WORTH MORE THAN THE ARM RESULT

Actual connecting per route is outturn_pax less p2p_outturn; each feed's error is
log(actual / feed), n=1,881:

  control    needs x1.28    IQR of log error 2.666
  QSI feed   needs x1.62    IQR of log error 2.840

An IQR of 2.666 in logs is a factor of FOURTEEN across the middle half of routes. Both feeds
are close to uninformative about how much connecting traffic an individual new route carries.
The open question is not which feed; it is whether route-level connecting traffic is
forecastable from this data at all. That belongs in how the connecting number is presented to
a client, not only in the backlog.

7. ONE THING ON THE SHIPPED PATH

The flat feed hands a connecting forecast to 567 of the 635 routes that carry none, 89% of
them, while under-reading volume by 28% where traffic exists. That is where a client's number
comes from today.

8. THREE OF MY OWN CONCLUSIONS, WITHDRAWN THE SAME DAY

  - that the flat feed was running LARGE and compensating for the P2P under-read. It needs
    x1.28, so it under-reads too. Dead.
  - that the QSI feed had a silent-zero defect on 22% of routes carrying connecting traffic.
    It is the P2P_CARRIERS list, and at least 419 of those 546 routes are the list.
  - that the zeros' 42.2% precision showed the QSI feed discriminating. It is the carrier
    list discriminating, which is why precision held across 944 firings instead of decaying
    to the 20.7% base rate.

The pattern in all three: reasoning from a mechanism before measuring it.

9. THE LESSON THAT SHOULD OUTLIVE THE DAY

A config object that is None in one arm and a dict in the other is not one switch, it is four.
Three behaviours in route_feed key off "if feed_cfg" rather than off the named flag, so any
flag that creates the object turns all of them on. Worth a sweep for the same shape elsewhere
before the next A/B is designed.
