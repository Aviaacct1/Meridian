W3: the fetch-set builder and the Commons coverage probe for airport photography

John's ruling of 19 September widens W3's imagery scope: mood frames and charts
stay, and airport-specific photography is added from online sources, with paid
stock held for the airports a search cannot cover, so a photograph exists for most
of the 300 plus airports at Routes in both the deck and the HTML pack.

Nobody knows what share of those airports Wikimedia Commons already covers at a
usable size and licence, and that number decides whether a second source is worth
building and whether stock is worth buying. So this is the measurement, not the
pipeline.

deck/build_image_set.py: generates the avia_images.py fetch-set input. The airports
come from the engine's own airport table ranked by size, because that is the
population a visitor at a route development show names; the airport name and city
come from the airport reference, because a subject built from an IATA code alone
finds nothing on Commons. Four slot types, terminal, airside, city skyline and city
centre, selectable. The six worked-route airports and the five meeting airports are
always included, Genoa among them at 1.85m passengers, because a small airport is
the case worth measuring. Codes absent from the reference are reported and left
out rather than guessed at.

deck/routes2026_probe.json: the generated probe, 401 airports, one terminal slot
each. Committed generated so the workstation installs nothing to run it.

Run on the workstation, dry-run, which searches and scores and downloads nothing.
Commons is unreachable from the DevPC mount's shell (the egress proxy refuses with
a 403), and avia_images.py states on its own first page that it runs on the
workstation and not in a sandbox.

No change to the engine, to cortex_app.py, or to any run path.

routes/W3-STATUS.md: the widened imagery scope logged for the controller, the
confidential-against-published split that decides how the pack resolves images,
the finding that C:\assets\engagement holds no manifest.json so the fetcher has
never written into it, and what happens after the probe reports.
