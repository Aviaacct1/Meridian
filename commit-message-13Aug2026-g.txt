Re-level the arm's connecting feed and cut the error, without a run

BOARDS-ARE-IDENTICAL closed the confound: CacheBoards and OagBoards return the same qshare
on every one of 40 pin routes, median 1.000 with min and max also 1.000, so qsi_k is
directly comparable across the two paths and the 16.67x gap is one quantity rather than two
incomparable ones.

That leaves two measurements standing against each other. LEVEL-VS-SHAPE has the arm at
k=0.06 needing x1.62 to reach actual connecting, which re-levels to an over-read of circa
10x at k=1.0. FEED-LEVELS has the live path at k=1.0 reading 0.93x of the 2025 analyst on
SJC-TPE. FLOOR-EVIDENCED offers the shape that might reconcile them: Sabre under-counts
transfer traffic by 2.07x on routes touching a major Asian hub and 2.50x on long haul over
6,000km, and SJC-TPE is both.

bt2/feed_level_recut.py asks whether the over-read has that shape. k is a pure multiplier,
so every figure is arithmetic on backtest_qsifeed_11Aug2026.csv: no engine, no store scan,
no refit. It reports, per cut, the multiplier the feed still needs and the k that would put
that cut's median route on the nose. Cuts by haul, by a named list of Asian and Gulf hubs as
the destination, by the arm's own hub_dest flag, by carrier type, by region and by cohort.

THE READING. If k needed climbs towards 1.0 on long haul and on the hub list while sitting
near 0.06 on short haul, the shipped level is right for the case it was tuned on and wrong
elsewhere, and one global k is the wrong instrument. If it is flat and low everywhere, the
shipped level is too high and the SJC-TPE agreement needs another explanation. The IQR
decides how much either reading is worth, because FEED-IS-UNINFORMATIVE recorded a spread of
a factor of fourteen across the middle half of routes and a level cannot fix a spread.

THE BASIS CAVEAT, in the tool's own docstring because it governs every figure it prints. The
arm built its feed at the route's actual flown departure time, block and frequency from the
pin wave cache. The live path builds it at the time the optimiser picks, which maximises
connecting passengers, on a modelled block and the requested frequency. So this measures
what the ARM's feed would read at k=1.0, not what the portal produces, and the optimiser
biases the live figure upward by construction. It is the shape of the error, not the level
the client is served.

Routes carrying no connecting traffic (635 of 3,072, per RULER-SOUND) and routes the
P2P_CARRIERS list zeroed are counted and never averaged in, since neither is a ratio.

Smoke-tested on a synthetic arm CSV before the real one, because the whole result is one
multiplication and a wrong scale would be invisible in the output. A route whose feed is
exactly right at k=1.0 returns ratio 1.000 and k needed 1.000; one that over-reads returns
0.004 on both; the zero-feed and no-connecting rows are skipped and counted.
