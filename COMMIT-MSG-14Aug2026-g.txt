Forecast pack: the fifteen pages a forecast run produces on its own

deck/forecast_pack.py. John's ruling of 14 August is one forecast per deck: a
person runs a forecast, optimises it, settles on one they like, and wants that
forecast as something sendable. The 40-page Observatory deck needs a research
pack; this needs only the contract.

The page list is a copy rather than a design. It is the forecast section of
China Airlines TPE-SJC Forecast 17Sep25.pptx, slides 6, 7 and 32 to 41, which is
a shape a client has already accepted, with a cover, a disclaimer and a summary
page in front of it. Cover, disclaimer, summary, competition at both airports,
the three addressable markets, the traffic forecast table, connecting at the hub,
connecting at the origin, the methodology divider and its four pages, this
forecast against the last, and the catchment.

Nothing in it computes a forecast. Every figure is read from the deck contract by
its own key, and the contract is checked by app/contract_legs_check.py before it
gets here.

Two pages fail open. Alliance seat share comes from the OAG store and not from
the contract, so without it that page is dropped: a competitive claim with no
measurement behind it is worse than no page. The comparison against a previous
forecast needs a previous contract, and a first run of a pair has none, so it is
dropped rather than drawn empty.

The summary page carries the basis of the run beside the answer: frequency,
gauge, seat count and its source, service year, connecting floor setting and the
departure time basis. All four contract defects corrected earlier today came from
a figure travelling without its basis.

The schedule page improves on the one it copies. Slide 37 of the 2025 deck says
the schedule "seeks to mitigate night curfew restrictions at SJC" and states no
figure, because none was computed. This one carries what the restriction costs.

Smoke tested on a hand-built contract: 13 pages, both conditional pages dropped
and named. The render path is untested and needs a run on the workstation.
