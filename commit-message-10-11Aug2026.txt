The forecast is for a year, the schedule is a choice, and the total lands on the analyst

Three additions on top of commit 58c04a6, which fixed the connecting feed. That commit made
the feed read correctly against the 2025 analyst. This one makes the TOTAL land, and gives
the tool the two things a planner asks it for: what year, and what time of day.

1. THE FORECAST YEAR, WITH A YEAR TO CHOOSE

Every Meridian output was a CURRENT-YEAR figure, because growth_years defaulted to zero,
while the client format forecasts a maturity year: the analyst's SJC-TPE deck is YE Jun 2028
grown 33% from a YE Jun 2025 base on a +16% / +7% / +7% build. Comparing the two directly
was comparing 2025 with 2028, and that is most of why Meridian read low on the total.

forecast_year now names the year wanted, on calibrated_forecast, on /api/forecast and
/api/optimise, and as a dropdown on the assessment screen. It DEFAULTS to the base data year
plus one, so with Sabre 2025 loaded the default output is 2026. An explicit growth or
growth_years from the caller still wins, so no existing call changes.

/api/basis is added so the page never hard-codes a year: it reports the Sabre base year, the
OAG week, the default forecast year and the ten years on offer, and the dropdown is built
from that. Refresh the store and the years move on their own.

THE GROWTH IS TAPERED, AND IT IS THE SAME TAPER THE PROJECTION BUILD ALREADY USED. The
measured two-year market CAGR on SJC-TPE reads 20.00%, which is the clamp ceiling and a
post-COVID recovery burst rather than a sustained rate; compounded flat over five years it
gives 2.5x and an absurd forecast. The rate is held for two years then decayed to a 3% long
run, which is the same shape as the analyst's own +16% / +7% / +7%. Two growth rules in one
file is how /api/forecast and /api/optimise came apart twice before, so there is one.

  forecast year   growth applied from 2025
  2026            +20.0%
  2027            +44.0%
  2028            +64.6%
  2030            +84.3%

2. THE CONNECTIVITY FLOOR IS NOW A SWITCH, DEFAULT UNCHANGED

split_share exists because the engine's leg split under-credited transfer traffic at non-US
hubs. That was true of the FLAT feed. It is no longer true of the QSI feed: measured against
the analyst on his own 12:00 schedule the raw QSI feed reads 1.04x his beyond and 0.83x his
behind, and the floor then rescales the reported beyond from 12,467 to 27,324 two-way. A
correction sized for a feed that under-read is an over-correction on one that does not.

feed_cfg["split_floor"], and split_floor on calibrated_forecast, DEFAULT TRUE so nothing
shipped changes. The floor is total-preserving, so it moves the split and not the headline:

  SJC-TPE CI 4x, analyst 12:00, Southwest a partner, two-way
    floor ON    P2P 43,096   connecting 52,776   total 95,872
    floor OFF   P2P 71,792   connecting 24,082   total 95,872

With the floor off the connecting total is 24,082 against the analyst's 25,999, or 0.93x.
With it on it is 52,776, or 2.03x. The back-test decides which ships; this exists so the two
can be measured rather than argued about.

FOUR TIMES WEEKLY IS RIGHT FOR LAUNCH AND WRONG FOR MATURITY. In 2026 it spills 3,664, so
the analyst sized year one correctly. By 2028 it turns away 43,082 two-way and the route fits
DAILY at 86.2% with nothing spilled, carrying 192,134. The 777-300ER at 5x is the gauge
alternative. That is the pitch: launch at 4x, build to daily by year three.

Note the mechanism, because it is the frequency switch doing real work: demand RISES with
frequency, 154,466 at 4x to 192,134 at 7x, because a better schedule wins a larger QSI share.
Capacity and demand are not independent on this route and the tool now models that.

an optimiser that sizes the aircraft on one basis while the forecast reports on another is
the /api/forecast against /api/optimise divergence this file has been caught by twice.

6. THE OAG BASIS IS NOW CHOSEN, NOT ACCIDENTAL

`SELECT max(week) FROM oag` is gone. `week` is a VARCHAR, so that was a STRING maximum: it
returned 2026-05-25 because the label sorts highest in the alphabet, not because anyone chose
it or because it is the newest by date.

It matters more than a tie-break, because the three label forms are not interchangeable:

    single week   2026-05-25          ALL SEVEN regions, week commencing
    monthly       2025-05             SIX regions, whole month, NO ASIA
    part-month    2025-05p01/p16      ASIA ONLY, the month in halves

Asia is exported in half-months while the rest come as whole months. So a `max(week)` landing
on a monthly label would silently drop Asia from every forecast and a route over Taipei would
lose most of its competition with nothing failing. Taipei serves 98 destinations on a week
label and 26 across the monthly labels alone.

cortex_app.resolve_oag_week takes AVIA_OAG_WEEK if set, else the newest label carrying the
FULL region set, and returns the region count with it. /api/basis reports the choice and the
reason. Measured on E:\Avia:

    default        2026-05-25, 7 regions, "newest of 51 complete-world labels"
    2025-05-26     honoured, 7 regions
    2025-05        honoured with "WARNING, 6 of 7 regions"
    2099-01        refused by name

Same answer as before, for a reason that can be checked. A partial 2026-06 extract can no
longer become the basis by arriving.

A FULL-YEAR basis is still not wired, deliberately. It is assemblable (monthly label plus its
p-labels gives seven regions) but days_of_op is a WEEKLY mask, so unioning it across twelve
months would inflate every frequency up to twelvefold, and frequency drives the QSI score
directly. That is its own piece of work and it is written up in the project folder as
"OAG basis - one week against a full year - 11Aug2026.md".

8. THE ASIA PART-MONTH LABELS ARE FOLDED, AND airportsdata IS PINNED

OAG exports Asia in half-months while the other six regions come as whole months, so the
store carried monthly labels with SIX regions and separate p01/p16/p23 labels with Asia only.
Three modules coded around it - bt2_capture falls back from p16 to p01, bt2_months classifies
"week LIKE '%p__'", fy_capacity notes two fortnights make a month - and it cost a wrong
reading on 11 August: the monthly labels alone show Taipei serving 26 destinations across a
year against 98 in one week, which first read as the store having no Asia at all.

Checked before recommending it: the halves are complementary in TIME, not overlapping. Cathay
451, Taipei to Hong Kong at 19:30 in May 2025, is 15 rows in p01 and 16 in p16, which is 31,
the days of May. Each row is a dated instance carrying the weekly mask that applied on that
date, and the reader dedupes by flight and unions the masks anyway.

bt2/migrate_oag_asia_labels.py folds them, --check first and --apply second, verifying row
count, that no part label survives, and that every parent month reaches the full region set,
then stamping _store_meta as the T-100 repair did. RUN AND VERIFIED on E:\Avia, 11 August:

    rows before 332,978,415, after 332,978,415        OK
    part-month labels remaining 0                     OK
    parent months short of 7 regions 0                OK
    53 labels folded, 27,397,091 rows, 26 months now complete worlds

The basis correctly did NOT move: 2026-05-25 is still the newest complete-world label, so the
engine behaves identically. Verified afterwards, 40 of 40.

airportsdata IS NOW PINNED at 20260803 in requirements.txt. It supplies every coordinate the
engine uses, so a release that corrects one airport shifts a great-circle distance, shifts the
circuity screen, and can add or drop a competing hub. Measured: the same commit on the same
stores returns qsi_share 0.2510 on 20260315 and 0.2513 on 20260803. Small, and a forecast
moving because a library was updated is not acceptable in a client number, so the version is
an input and sits beside the store vintage. The acceptance test keys the expected share off
the installed release and says which, rather than hiding both behind a wide tolerance - this
file already carries three cases of a scoring basis drifting because a band was loosened.
bt2/coord_check.py names which airport moved when two machines disagree.

9. TWO ADDITIONS OF 12 AUGUST, BOTH IN FILES ALREADY IN THIS COMMIT

app/backtest.py ALSO CARRIES gcd_km_many, which is not described above and is the largest
runtime fix in the commit. gcd_km full-scanned 332m rows once per pair, measured at 29.6
seconds a scan, and the --min-gcd filter called it once per route: 14,015 sequential scans,

Recorded here because a reader of this message would otherwise not know the fix is in it.

run-backtest-11Aug2026.ps1 IS REVISED. As written on 11 August it omitted --jobs (which
defaults to 1, so all three arms would have run single-process), --preagg (so every route
would have full-scanned Sabre), and --routes-file. The third is a correctness fault rather
than a speed one: backtest.py records at line 928 that discovery does not return identical
membership run to run, 145 routes against 83 on the case that proved it, and
compare_backtest_arms.py pairs on the routes ALL arms scored. Three unpinned discoveries
would have shrunk the paired sample invisibly. The runbook now discovers once with
--discover-only, pins the set, and runs all three arms against the pin.

The launch years move with it, 2016-2019 to 2016,2017,2018,2024. 2024 is added because the
six-year wave cache covers it and a post-COVID cohort bears directly on a connecting feed
whose shape changed across the break; 2025 cannot be, since --offset 1 would grade it
against 2026 and Sabre stops at 2025. 2019 is DROPPED because --offset 1 was grading it
against 2020 OUTTURN. The --years help calls 2016-2019 the clean sample with 2020-2023
excluded, but that excludes them as LAUNCH years and not as GRADING years, and --min-outturn
then removes the worst-hit routes from the statistics, so the surviving 2019 cohort is
selected on having come through COVID well. The four cohorts now grade against 2017, 2018,
2019 and 2025, every one a normal year.
