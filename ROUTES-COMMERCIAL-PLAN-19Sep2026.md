# World Routes 2026: commercial plan for Meridian

19 September 2026. Companion to `HANDOVER-ROUTES-19Sep2026.md` (engineering and stand flow).
This document covers everything else: sales strategy, the presentation and the live output,
the stand host's brief and manual, pricing, leads and feedback, the website, pre-show marketing,
meetings, and the competitor question. Owners are named; decisions marked JOHN are his alone.
Figures are deliberately absent where they are decisions: nothing here invents a price.

---

## 1. What Routes is for, in one sentence

Not to close sales. To leave the hall with a list of named, qualified people who have seen their
own route run live, hold a pack in their inbox with their numbers in it, and have agreed to a
follow-up conversation; and with three to five of them lined up as year-1 paid beta clients.
Everything below serves that sentence. A visitor who sees a beautiful demo and leaves without a
pack and a next step was a cost.

---

## 2. Sales strategy: the funnel from stand to signature

1. **Attract** (pre-show and on the day): messaging (section 8), the stand's one hook ("run your
   route now, on the stand, in seconds"), the imagery, the host's opening line.
2. **Demonstrate** (2-5 minutes): Run on their route; Optimise for the interested. The stand flow
   in the handover, section 4.
3. **Capture** (60 seconds, never skipped): the request form. Name, company, role, email, route,
   airline, what they are pitching at Routes, consent. Section 6.
4. **Deliver** (30 minutes): the researched pitch pack emailed, with the cover note carrying the
   next step. Section 3.
5. **Follow up** (48 hours, then 2 weeks): John or Jol, personally, with the beta one-pager for
   the qualified. Section 5.
6. **Convert** (November-December): beta agreements, onboarding, hosting per client.

Qualification on the stand, three questions the host asks naturally: Are you pitching routes
here this week (yes = airport, the core buyer)? Do you build your own forecasts today, or buy
them (buy = budget exists)? Who signs off a tool like this at your organisation (names the real
buyer for the follow-up)?

## 3. The presentation, the pack, and the imagery

### 3.1 Three artefacts, one look
- **Stand deck** (PowerPoint, 10 slides, for the host and for the pre-arranged meetings):
  problem, what Meridian does in the three-classes-of-number framing, accuracy stated exactly as
  ruled (calibrated leads, blind only as portfolios), two worked routes with real charts, the
  product family, beta offer on the last slide. Observatory palette, Avia metadata, en-GB.
- **The live pack** (HTML, opened on the stand laptop or in the visitor's browser): the
  researched pitch the tool already generates, tuned for a stand conversation: the route on a
  map, the market background, the forecast, the time-of-day curve, the tail chart, the
  economics, every figure with its source line. The host can walk an airline through it on the
  stand for a route already run.
- **The PDF** of the same pack, for the email and for printing: the airline person at Routes
  wants something they can forward. A PDF render of the HTML pack (headless Chrome on the
  workstation) is a build item: same content, same imagery, A4, Avia author metadata.

### 3.2 Imagery that makes it look the part
The Observatory imagery library (102MB, rights manifests, at `C:\assets` on the Dev PC and
`D:\assets` on the workstation, configured through `ASSETS_DIR`) is exactly for this: airport
and aircraft photography cleared for use. Rules: every image used carries its rights record;
the PNG-to-JPEG conversion that strips provenance from delivered decks (estate list, open) is
fixed or worked around before any pack goes to a visitor; the hero image per pack is chosen by
route end (destination airport where the library has one) so each pack looks made for them.
The stand backdrop and the deck draw on the same library so the look is one look.

### 3.3 What must be true of every pack
One-run definition (the pack carries the numbers the visitor saw). Source line on every figure.
No "illustrative" label anywhere. The accuracy wording exactly as ruled. The economics
disclaimer present. The cover note names the host and John and asks for the follow-up.

## 4. The stand host: brief and training manual

Produce as one document, `STAND-HOST-MANUAL.md`, then a printed copy. Contents:

1. **Who we are, in 90 seconds.** Avia: independent since 2001, senior operators not a bench,
   the transactions and forecasts that prove it. Meridian: 25 years of QSI practice made into a
   tool, calibrated against real launches.
2. **The three classes of number.** Measured, calibrated, physics-capped. If the host can
   explain this, they can answer most methodology questions honestly; if they cannot answer,
   the line is "John can take you through the method in detail, shall I set that up".
3. **The demo script**, timed: opening line; Run on the visitor's route (what to point at as it
   lands: the market background, the capture, the curve); Optimise for the interested (what it
   is doing while it fills); the pack, explained with the two pre-built examples; the request
   form; the close. Two rehearsed routes for walk-ups with no route in mind.
4. **What not to say.** No feature not in the frozen build. No price beyond "beta pricing on
   request, limited places". No single-route blind accuracy figure. No comparison with a named
   competitor by us; if raised, section 10's line.
5. **Plan B**, step by step: when the network drops, what to do, in what order, and the phone
   numbers.
6. **The queue view**: how to see a pack was sent, and what to do when one fails.
7. **Objections and answers**, a page: "we have our own model", "how accurate is it really",
   "where does the data come from", "can it do day-of-week / fares" (honest: on the roadmap),
   "what does it cost".
8. **The day**: setup, breaks, who is on the stand when, end-of-day lead review with John.

Training: two half-days on 14-16 October against the frozen build, with the Boeing demo
recording as reference; a timed dry run on the 16th with Jol playing a sceptical airport.

## 5. Pricing (JOHN)

Structure only; the numbers are John's, after the licence gate (handover, section 7).

- **Gate first**: Sabre and OAG licence position on selling derived outputs, in writing. If
  restrictive, Meridian is priced as a service Avia delivers (routes per month, packs, review
  calls), not as tool access. Both structures below survive that; the pitch wording changes.
- **Three tiers, no more**: Airport (route development team, N users, N routes per month,
  packs, one review call per quarter); Airline (network planning, same shape, more routes);
  Adviser (consultancies and lessors; per-seat, higher route volume, no packs branded Avia).
- **Year-1 beta**: a stated discount off list for a fixed number of firms (five is enough), in
  return for feedback, a reference if satisfied, and tolerance on hosting while it settles.
  Year 2 at list less a smaller, stated loyalty discount. Both discounts written down; the
  point of beta pricing is that it visibly ends.
- **Anchor**: price against what a QSI route study costs bought from Avia as consultancy
  today, which John knows. The tool must be cheaper per route and dearer per year, or nobody
  will believe either number.
- **Never on the stand**: a price. "On request, limited beta places" is the whole answer, and
  the one-pager goes out in the follow-up, not in the pack.

## 6. Leads, CRM and feedback

### 6.1 One lead store, from the first day
Every request-form entry, every business card, every pre-arranged meeting, in one table:
name, company, role, email, phone, route(s) run, airline(s), pitching-at-Routes, buyer named,
interest (cold / warm / beta candidate), pack sent (yes, when, failures), follow-up owner,
follow-up dates, notes. The request form writes to it automatically (handover 4.2); cards and
meetings are typed in by the host at end of day. Exported to Excel nightly to Egnyte so nothing
depends on the workstation staying up. Whether that table is the workstation DuckDB or a
lightweight CRM (a free HubSpot tier would do) is JOHN's call; the requirement is one table,
written to by the form, exported nightly, and readable on a phone.

### 6.2 Feedback, captured while it is fresh
- **On the stand**: five questions the host answers after each substantive demo, on a card or
  the same form: what route, what impressed, what they questioned, what they asked for that we
  do not have, would they pay. Thirty seconds per visitor; the aggregate is the product
  roadmap for November.
- **In the pack email**: one line inviting a reply ("does this match what you have run?"); the
  replies are the most honest feedback we will get.
- **After Routes**: the tester feedback channel already in use (Jarek, Antonio) extended to
  every beta client, and every item goes onto `MASTER-TASK-LIST.md`, not into an inbox.

form. A stand without a URL that captures loses every visitor who did not fill the form.

We do not know who is interested; we know who should be. Choose by three criteria, in this
order: an existing Avia relationship (the meeting gets accepted); a route development team
that pitches at Routes (the core buyer); an unserved long-haul question we can run before the

- The three Taipei airlines already shown the curve (EVA, China Airlines, Starlux): a

Ask for the meetings from 22 September; late requests at Routes are rarely honoured. Each
meeting: their route pre-run, the pack printed, Optimise live, the beta one-pager left behind.
Five meetings and fifty stand conversations is a good show.

## 10. The competitor question (PaxUp and others)

JOHN's decision, with a view: **do not approach a competitor's clients for a comparison before
launch.** Three reasons. It invites a feature-by-feature comparison against a mature product
at exactly the moment Meridian has known gaps (day-of-week, fare, the new types' economics)
and one shared password; it signals that we define ourselves against them; and the feedback
would be about their tool, not about whether ours solves the buyer's problem. The comparison
conversation is worth having once a paid-beta client exists and can speak for us.

What to do instead, now: know the competitor's positioning cold (their public pages, their
Routes presence, their pricing model if published) so the host and John can answer "how is
this different" in one honest sentence without naming them first. The sentence should rest on
what is verifiably ours: the calibration against real launches with the accuracy ruling
behind it, the time-of-day optimisation, the researched pack, and 25 years of doing this by
hand. If a visitor is a competitor's client and volunteers it, the host listens, captures what
they like and miss, and offers the pack; that is feedback arriving for free, without us
soliciting it.

Nothing about any competitor goes in the deck, the pack, the website, or any post.
