The sensitivity run raised the scored population 73%, from 2,871 launches to
4,963, and the centre did not move: median 0.778 in both runs, p75 0.841 in both,
share above 0.875 at 14.5% then 14.4%. The conclusion does not depend on the
filters.

Corrects PLAN-LF-MEASURED, which said haul does nothing and had measured only the
centre. The medians are still within 2.0 points across the four haul cuts, but
the share above 0.875 is monotonic in the larger population, 11.6% to 17.8%, and
p90 runs 0.883 to 0.907. Short haul has a thinner upper tail. A cap lives in the
tail, so the centre was the wrong statistic for the question.

Exact percentiles size the prize. At a constant 85.5th percentile the cap would be
0.862 to 0.888 across the marginal cuts, a range whose midpoint is 0.875 exactly,
and 0.853 to 0.900 across the ten crossed cells with 30 or more routes. Long haul
clusters at 0.881 to 0.892. So the differences are 4 to 7 points at the median and
2.0 to 4.7 at the 85th percentile: the tails converge where the middles do not.

Keeps one global cap at 0.875. Worst case from doing so is 2.5 points of load
factor, 2.9% of the carried total, on the 15.6% of routes where the cap binds,
which is about an eighth of the plus or minus 20% tolerance on one route in six.

The deciding argument is not the size. A per-cell cap fitted on achieved load
factors would raise the ULCC medium-haul cap to 0.900, and CAP-IS-LOAD-BEARING
measured 422 of the 818 pinned routes still over-forecasting by more than 20%
with the cap holding them down. The cap is absorbing a demand over-read, so
tuning it to observed load factors treats it as what it is labelled rather than
what it does.

The measurement is therefore the right basis for schedule_sizing.PLANNING_LF,
which is a commercial statement about how full a schedule is written to fly, and
the wrong basis for the engine brake. The brake stays global and labelled until
the over-read it absorbs is fixed at source.
