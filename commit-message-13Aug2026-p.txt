The target sweep, and the control the sector result must survive

THE THREE BASES ON ONE SAMPLE, relaxed, six cohorts, only the grading target moving.

  blind, route level      nonstop 60.9%   p2p_outturn 61.0%   sector 78.5%
  long-haul intl FSC              39.8%               40.2%          73.7%
  short-haul dom or LCC           72.6%               72.5%          83.3%
  calibrated +-20%                83.2%               85.4%          89.3%
  portfolios of 20                93.2%               94.1%          98.8%
  n                               6,524               6,524          6,478

nonstop and p2p_outturn exclude exactly the same 286 artifacts of 6,810, so the population
is identical and only the definition moved. Sector excludes 332 and scores on 46 fewer
routes, which is what counting the connecting feed on the leg should do.

ON THE CLIENT'S OWN NUMBER THE CLAIM IS BETTER, NOT WORSE, and the segment Avia actually
pitches moves from 39.8% to 73.7%.

THAT RE-READS BASIS-COSTS-33-POINTS. Holding population and grading year fixed and moving
only the target takes the blind figure up by 17.6 points, so the 33 points lost against the
pin cannot be the target. Population and grading year carry all of it and more. The pin
score said the same independently on its own sample: 28.9% against total outturn versus
22.4% against pure P2P, the larger denominator scoring better.

THE TWO LOCAL DEFINITIONS ARE NOT THE SAME QUANTITY. p2p_outturn scores above nonstop by 2.2
points calibrated at +-20% and 0.1 blind at route level, on an identical population. Small,
and it matters because the pin grades fc_over_p2p against the connecting-column definition
while the model trains on the itinerary label.

AND THE SECTOR RESULT MUST NOT TRAVEL WITHOUT A CONTROL. The model predicts passengers per
seat and multiplies by seats_ly. Sector traffic on a leg is close to seats times an achieved
load factor, which sits in a narrow band on almost every route in the world, while the local
market's share of those same seats runs from a few per cent to nearly all of them. A target
that is nearly a load factor is easier to hit within +-20% than a target that is a share, so
part of the +17.6 could be a property of the quantity rather than of the model.

bt2/null_model_control.py forecasts every route as seats_ly times the median ratio of the
other cohorts, leave one cohort out, scored exactly as bt2_claimset scores the model, and
reports the target's own dispersion beside it. On synthetic distributions shaped like each
target, and this is illustration rather than measurement, a constant scores 84.5% on a
load-factor-like target and 18.5% on a share-like one. If the real null lands anywhere near
the model, the sector claim is arithmetic on seats and the model's contribution is the
distance above the floor rather than the figure itself.
