Join the forecast to the Observatory deck: the adapter that never existed

John was right and I had scoped "wired in" too narrowly. The 40-page Observatory deck is
real, the forecast is real, and nothing connected them. run_observatory_pitch.py says so in
its own docstring: "deck_contract.py is not yet wired into the connector, so the fc handed in
here carries route identity and placeholder demand only. Nothing in the deck reads a number
from it. Do not read the output as a forecast of anything."

And /api/report does not build the Observatory deck at all. It imports route_deck, which is
the summary PPTX and describes itself in its own header as a hand-typed summary. So the deck
a client sees and the forecast the tool computes have never met.

Everything downstream of the contract already works: deck_contract emits it, forecast_spec
translates it, spec_from_research renders it. The break is one join, and deck_contract still
expects the shape assess.py produced in the June chain.

app/forecast_to_contract.py maps the calibrated_forecast payload onto the two dicts
build_contract takes. THREE MAPPINGS ARE NOT OBVIOUS and each is an error already paid for:

  CARRIED AGAINST DEMAND. The payload carries both. demand.total is carried after the 87.5%
  plan cap; demand.total_demand is uncapped. CAPPED-VS-UNCAPPED records a claim quoted for a
  week that compared one against the other. directional_demand takes the carried figure and
  the uncapped one travels beside it under its own name.

  SEATS. deck_contract._seats_for falls back to the generic AIRCRAFT table, where the A350-900
  is 336. China Airlines flies it at 306. The forecast's own seat count is passed as a
  cabin_config with the type's business fraction scaled to it, so the deck can never be sized
  8 to 13% above the forecast it describes.

  EACH WAY. The payload is each way throughout and nothing here doubles.

The selftest passes all seven checks against the real aircraft table, including 306 against
336.

app/deck_from_cases.py runs a cases file, the same one scenario_runner takes so a deck and a
scenario table cannot come from two definitions of one case, and emits a contract per route.
--report-only writes nothing and lists which blocks carry figures and which are empty,
aggregated across the set. That list is the answer to "I may need to come back and get the
tables populated": deck_contract emits a gap as None with a _need note rather than a zero, so
knowing the list before building a deck is the difference between populating tables and
discovering them.
