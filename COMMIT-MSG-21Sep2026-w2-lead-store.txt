W2: the lead store, built to the four record types John set out on 21 September

Adds app/lead_store.py and app/test_lead_store.py. Ruling 15 called for a DuckDB
leads table to replace the JSONL; John widened the scope on 21 September to the four
kinds of record a stand actually produces, and this is that store.

  route   someone ran a forecast: what they came to look at, the questions asked, what
          arose, pricing interest, and the exact run shown
  card    a business card drop, a photograph and two taps
  note    a remark worth keeping from someone who left no details. Not a lead at all:
          market intelligence, and the least collected thing at any trade stand
  voice   the host's own dictated impressions

One table with a kind column rather than four tables, because the records share a spine
and have to leave together as one flat sheet: the follow-up work after Routes is sorting
and filtering, not joining. An append-only lead_events log sits beside the rows, keeping
the one real virtue of the JSONL it replaces, and lead_files carries photographs, voice
notes and packs.

SEVEN PROPERTIES, each a check rather than an intention:

  the record survives the pack   capture happens first and depends on no send
  editable after the moment      the useful detail arrives once the visitor has gone
  notes append, never replace    a second thought does not erase the first
  the log is append-only         what happened stays answerable after a correction
  the provider is the authority  provider_message_id, provider_status, reconcile()
  the quota is John's 16 Aug     first pack free, the rest held, a failure stays free
  migration refuses to lose      unreadable lines are counted, not dropped

The fifth exists because of this morning. On 21 September our own data would have said
three packs were sent and the provider had never heard of them, and nothing we stored
could have contradicted it, because we only stored what we believed. reconcile() takes a
fetcher, asks the provider about every message we think we sent, writes the answer back,
and returns the ones the provider does not recognise. A stand promising a pack within
thirty minutes has to be able to tell sent from believed-sent.
