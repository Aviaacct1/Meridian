The aircraft turns: arrival and departure tied by a turnaround, by flight type

JOHN, 14 August: "The arrival time in SJC still does not match with the departure time
from SJC. These should be tied together. with an intl intercontinental average turnaround
and for LATAM flights for a continental intl turnaround time and for dom a dom turnaround
average."

THIS IS THE THIRD MODEL AND THE FIRST CORRECT ONE. The shipped code derived the return as
arrival plus a fixed turn, which models a shuttle. I then searched forward for the earliest
legal return, which maximised the sit at the outstation. I then searched backward from a
three-hour target, which was closer but still treated the arrival as free. It is not free:
an aircraft that departs the origin at T ARRIVED at T minus the turnaround. The two
movements are one rotation. The long gap belongs at the DESTINATION, where the aircraft
flies another route, which is what John established earlier.

AND IT CONSTRAINS THE DEPARTURE, WHICH IS THE PART THAT MATTERS. A departure whose implied
arrival falls inside a restriction cannot be flown. On SJC-TPE against a 21:00-06:00 curfew
the optimiser chose 06:30, which needs the aircraft on stand from 03:30, three and a half
hours inside the restriction. It was choosing schedules nobody can operate.

app/route_feed.py
  turnaround_mins(): domestic 60, continental international 90, intercontinental 180, by
  country match and a 3,000km continental threshold. Overridable per case with
  feed_cfg["turnaround_mins"]. STATED AS PLANNING AVERAGES, NOT MEASUREMENTS, so they can
  be argued with: a widebody off a thirteen-hour sector needs deep cleaning, full catering,
  a crew change and a fuel uplift, and a domestic narrowbody turns in under an hour.
  optimise_departure.permitted() now screens the arrival that feeds the departure against
  the origin's hours, and the hub departure that fed THAT against the destination's.

app/cortex_app.py
  _schedule_times derives the return arrival as the departure minus the turnaround and
  stops searching. Where the implied arrival or the return departure falls inside a
  restriction it draws the schedule and NAMES the conflict, rather than moving the times to
  hide a departure that should have been rejected where departures are chosen.
  The turnaround is computed once per run from the two countries and the great circle, put
  into feed_cfg, and passed to both the optimiser and the schedule builder, so they cannot
  disagree.

Measured: SJC-TPE 180 min, LHR-JFK 180, SCL-LIM 90 (the LATAM case), SJC-AUS 60.
With a 21:00-06:00 curfew and a 180-minute turn the permitted departures are 09:00 to
20:30, the earliest fed by an arrival at 06:00 as the restriction lifts. The same curfew on
a domestic route with a 60-minute turn permits 07:00 to 20:30. Before this change the
optimiser was free to pick 06:00 to 20:30 on both.
