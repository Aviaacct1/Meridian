Task 1 harness, and the wiring fault it found before anything was wired

WHAT THIS COMMIT DOES NOT DO. It does not wire the calibrated model into cortex_app. Task 3 is
held, and the reason is in the first section below.

THE WIRING IS NOT PROVED. app/route_context.py line 194 sets capa, the model's third feature,
to the engine's qsi_share. That is not the quantity the model was trained on. Training capa is
bt2_capture.cap_from line 86: qns / (qns + qcx), where qns is the proposed nonstop's frequency
times an elapsed-time penalty and qcx is 0.20 x (online + 0.75 alliance + 0.25 interline) over
the one-stop itineraries between the same two airports, averaged over the two directions. It is
a nonstop-versus-connecting share of the pair's own service. The engine's qsi_share is the share
of the catchment's traffic to the destination won at the origin airport, which is a different
quantity with a different meaning and a different scale.

MEASURED, not argued. Training capa on the four pin cohorts, n=4,287: median 0.9168, p10 0.4896,
p90 1.0000. The engine's qsi_share reads 0.059 at daily on SJC-TPE against a measured airport
factor of 0.32. So the live path would feed feature 3 a value below the tenth percentile of
training on essentially every route, silently, and the published accuracy would describe nothing
the client is shown.

Two more of the twenty-one inputs are built by different code in the live path than in training
and neither has been compared. qcx sums BOTH directions in training, bt2_lib line 60; route_context
line 207 builds it from one set of engine sums. legs_n in training is every OAG leg touching either
endpoint in the pre-launch week; the live value is the connection-set leg count from route_qsi.

bt2_wiring_test.py did not catch this and could not have. It proved that bt2_forecast._vec and
bt2_g12_exp.X_of build the same vector FROM THE SAME INPUTS, feeding both from the training rows.
It never called route_context.

THE FIX IS ONE IMPLEMENTATION RATHER THAN TWO: load_legs, components and cap_from move into app/
and both the training chain and the live path import them. That is John's ruling of 9 August
applied to the three inputs it was not applied to.

THE TWO PUBLISHED FIGURES ARE NOT A COMPARISON, and it is not only the sample. Population: the
calibrated rule takes virgin pairs, at least 1,500 nonstop passengers in the launch year and under
500 in each of the two years before, with a market floor and a capacity-ratio ceiling; the pin is
OAG new services at a minimum frequency of 3, a minimum sector of 1,500 km and a minimum outturn of
3,000. Target: the calibrated model is graded against Sabre itinerary='NON-STOP' passengers on the
pair, bt2_discover line 64, which is the local market and excludes every connecting passenger,
while fc_over_out is graded against sector traffic, backtest lines 246 to 264, which counts the
connecting feed. Year: launch year against launch year plus one.

So the published 92% and 86% describe a LOCAL NONSTOP forecast, not the sector total a client is
shown. That has to be settled before the calibrated layer produces the client number, because
otherwise an accuracy claim is attached to a different quantity from the one it was measured on.

MEASURED ON THE DEV PC, 12 August 2026

  The calibrated model refit leave-one-cohort-out on the pin's four cohorts only, 2016/2017/2018/
  2024, relaxed sample, Sabre throughout: 59.8% within +-20%, n=4,287, in 14 seconds on two cores.
  Against the published 60.4%, which uses six cohorts and regrades US domestic launches onto the
  DOT. Dropping 2019 and 2025 and staying on Sabre costs about a point.

  THE MODEL DOES NOT WEAKEN IN THIN MARKETS, which bears on the leakage retrain. By base market:
  under 500 66.9%, 500-1,000 59.7%, 1,000-2,000 61.1%, 2,000-5,000 62.7%, 5,000-20,000 58.1%,
  above 20,000 53.1%. The thin end is its best band. The 250 floor is a property of the discovery
  rule, not of the model's competence, so widening the rule is the change Task 2 needs and the
  domain guard's premise is narrower than it reads.
