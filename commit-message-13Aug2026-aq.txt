Fill the fields build_contract hardcodes to None, which no argument could reach

64% to 75%, six of the eight expected fields closed, and the two that did not are not gaps in
the model. deck_contract sets origin_city_code and destination_city_code to a literal None in
the dict at lines 382 and 383, with a _need note beside them, so passing them in `case`
reaches nothing at all and the first adapter did exactly that. The same shape at lines 398
and 402 for both schedule legs' dep_time and arr_time.

They are now filled in _fill_hardcoded, after the contract is built, and only where the
payload genuinely carries the figure. A field the payload cannot supply is left as
deck_contract wrote it, so its _need note still reads and the gap report still counts it.

THE SCHEDULE TIMES CARRY THEIR BASIS. They come from cortex_app._schedule_times, whose own
docstring calls them illustrative: not curfew-, slot- or connection-optimised. A deck
printing a departure time that an airline reads as a proposal, with no note, is worse than
one printing none. So the _need line is rewritten rather than cleared: "indicative" where the
payload says so, "departure time set by the caller" where the caller named one. The basis
travels onto the block instead of being dropped at the boundary, which is the rule dep_basis
already follows on the portal page.

breakeven_load_factor is closed: the economics module produces it as breakeven_lf and
deck_contract does not read it.

cask is deliberately left alone. It is cost per available seat kilometre, the payload carries
no such figure, and deriving it would mean choosing between an annual and a per-departure
cost basis on a line nobody would check. That is the choice that has gone wrong seven times
today, so it stays a named gap rather than a guessed number.
