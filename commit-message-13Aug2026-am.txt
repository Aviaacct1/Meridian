The report endpoint could not build the agreed SJC-TPE case

/api/report is the deck and workbook path, and its signature took aircraft, freq and
growth_years and nothing else of what a client case actually turns on. A deck built from it
for SJC-TPE would have used a generic A21X at 7x weekly, no named partner, no forecast year
and the default growth taper, which GROWTH-IS-THE-GAP measured at a 20.00% CAGR, the clamp
ceiling and a post-COVID recovery burst.

The agreed ladder runs a 306-seat A350-900 at 4x with Southwest counted as a partner, at 2027
or 2028, on post-recovery growth. So the deck and the scenario table would have described the
same route and disagreed by tens of thousands of passengers, which is worse than having no
deck.

SIX PARAMETERS ADDED, each one something a client case turns on:

  seats          the carrier's own configuration each way from OAG, not the generic table.
                 China Airlines flies the A350-900 at 306 against the table's 336
  partners       codes counted as commercial partners on the connecting feed. A partnership
                 is a fact about a deal, not a property of a schedule
  forecast_year  the maturity year the client is shown
  growth         an explicit market growth path, per John's ruling that client work states
                 its own rather than taking the default taper
  split_floor    the connectivity floor; 1 is what ships
  dep_time       HH:MM or HHMM. Omitted, the optimiser picks the time for the airline

dep_time is parsed by name rather than leniently. The first version stripped every colon, so
"12:0:0" became "1200" and answered 12:00, and "1:2:3" would have answered 01:23: a malformed
input producing a plausible time, and a departure time silently wrong by hours moves the whole
connecting feed. It now takes at most one colon, digits only, hours 0 to 23 and minutes 0 to
59, and returns a 400 naming what it received. Tested on thirteen forms including 2500, 1265,
"noon" and the empty string, which correctly means the optimiser chooses.
