US domestic routes state passenger counts each way, international stays two way

John, 22 August: US domestic route traffic is conventionally quoted each way, the DOT/T-100
enplanement basis every US carrier and airport already reports in. International traffic, SJC-TPE
included, is conventionally quoted two way, the basis deck_contract.py was already built on. The two
conventions were never going to sit on one document without confusion, so the basis is now keyed off
the route itself and applied once, never re-decided market by market inside a table - the design
constraint John set before any code was written: "if we key it off the route then we need to make the
basis of the data super clear."

CLASSIFIER. forecast_to_contract.case_and_outputs sets case["domestic"] = both fc.origin.country and
fc.dest.country equal "US". One comparison, one place. A caller that doesn't supply it (build_contract
used directly, without going through this adapter) gets today's existing two-way behaviour by default,
so nothing already live changes unless it opts in.

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES. build_contract() is left computing everything two way, exactly as it always
has, proven correct through the 14 and 20 August fixes - natural, p2p_carried, cnx_carried, carried,
the connecting city figures, none of that maths moved. A new _disp() wrapper is applied ONLY at the
points that print a passenger count into the contract's demand-summary fields: segment_forecast's
four blocks, connecting_at_hub/connecting_at_destination's cities and totals, top_markets_beyond_hub,
and the catchment/summary market-size headlines. On a domestic contract these come out at exactly
half; on international, unchanged.

WHAT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT CHANGE. Capacity (annual_seats/annual_capacity), the physical schedule
(outbound/inbound/total rows), revenue (pax_rev_y1, cargo, total_rev_y1), yield (ASK, PRASK, TRASK,
CASK, RPK) and average fares stay two way regardless of route type. These are operational and
financial facts, not a passenger-count accounting convention - the exact reasoning that held
cortex_workbook.py's Economics tab out of the EW/2-way tab pairing earlier the same day. Wrapping
proven two-way maths at the display edge, rather than re-deriving it two ways in parallel, was the
deliberate choice: one set of numbers, one place they could go wrong, not two.

PDEW/PTEW STAYS A RATE. pdew() takes a new mult parameter (default 2, unchanged for every existing
caller) so its own departures denominator scales with the SAME basis as the passenger figure it
divides - domestic calls pass mult=1. Confirmed by test: PTEW is identical whether the same route is
built domestic or international, exactly as a rate should read, matching capture_rate, CAGR and
every other ratio in the tool.

LABELLED, NOT LEFT IMPLICIT. contract["_demand_basis"] states "each way (US domestic)" or "two way"
at the contract root, so any consumer reads one canonical field rather than re-deriving domestic-ness
itself. The same string is repeated on route_metadata.catchment_headline, summary_and_schedule,
connecting_at_hub and connecting_at_destination, and grand_total's own _basis note - every block that
carries a _disp()'d figure says so on itself.

FOUND WHILE BUILDING THIS, TWO BUGS ALREADY FIXED EARLIER TODAY UNDER SEPARATE COMMITS: pdew()'s
flat-728 departures assumption (deck_contract-basis-fixes commit) and the each-way cities-list left
out of the "everything doubles here" rule (same commit). This commit is the domestic/international
feature itself, building on both.

VERIFICATION. test_deck_contract_ptew.py extended: builds the SAME route twice, once domestic once
international, and checks every _disp()'d field comes out at exactly half; PTEW and capture_rate are
identical between the two (rates, unaffected by basis); annual_capacity, revenue_forecast.passengers,
avg_ow_fare_blended and the physical schedule are byte-identical between the two (the deliberate
carve-out); _demand_basis states correctly on both; the 14 August LEGS SUM and SUBTOTAL invariants
hold on both. 27/27 pass. Both a domestic and an international workbook were built end to end through
emit_workbook() with no error. The ba_lhr_sjc_reference() acceptance-test fixture was rebuilt and
re-checked against its own published figures (82,708 P2P forecast, PDEW 113.6) - byte-identical,
confirming zero regression to the validated 2015 deck numbers this whole file is graded against.

NOT DONE: route_deck.py's own labelling has not been wired to this. Its forecast dict shape (market/
market_2w/total/total_2w) does not obviously map onto this contract's structure, and its docstring
does not mention deck_contract.py at all - unconfirmed whether it is even fed by the contract this
commit touches, versus forecast_pack.py or pitch_report.py, the other documented consumers. Needs
tracing which renderer actually builds a live domestic-route deck before wiring labels in, rather
than guessing at a shape that may not be the live path.
