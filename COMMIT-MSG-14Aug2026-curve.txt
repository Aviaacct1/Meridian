The departure-time curve, and a departure picker instead of a text box

JOHN, 14 August: "since optimise is looking at all options to decide best. Would it be
possible to create a chart that shows the total two way over the course of 24 hours as a
chart... That means that SJC could see easily if they want to choose a flight between say
10am and 2pm, what would be the time to choose."

THE CURVE ALREADY EXISTED AND WAS THROWN AWAY. optimise_departure scores every candidate
departure across the day into `tried` and reported len(tried), a count. The scores went in
the bin. An airport asking what a preferred slot window costs them is asking to read that
curve, and returning it costs nothing.

app/route_feed.py
  info["curve"]: every candidate as dep, hhmm, total, beyond, behind and PERMITTED, so a
  restricted hour can be distinguished from a genuine dip. Also info["turnaround_mins"].
  return_arrival corrected. It was best + 2 x block + turn, where the aircraft would land
  if it shuttled. It turns at the origin instead, so the inbound landed one turnaround
  before this departure: best - turn.

app/cortex_dashboard.html
  The chart, drawn on every run that optimises a departure. Restricted hours shaded, both
  halves of a window that crosses midnight handled; departures the aircraft cannot fly
  marked, including those whose inbound would land inside a restriction; the chosen time
  and, where a restriction moved it, the unrestricted optimum, both marked.
  WHAT IT SHOWS IS STATED UNDER IT: connecting demand at each departure time, each way,
  before capture scaling and before the capacity cap. Local demand does not vary with
  departure time anywhere in the engine, which WEIGHT-IS-A-NULL-TEST established, so the
  route total moves one for one with this curve until the aircraft fills. That is what
  makes it readable as a total, and saying so is the difference between a chart and a
  claim.
  The departure input is a quarter-hour dropdown rather than a text box, for the reason the
  curfew became one: a client asking for a specific time should not be able to introduce a
  format error into the run. Blank still means optimise, and both readers of the field are
  unchanged because a select yields .value exactly as an input did.

STILL OPEN: the turnaround figures. 60, 90 and 180 minutes by flight type are planning
averages with no source, and John's suggestion of an AIRCRAFT TYPE average is better than a
regional one, since it is the aeroplane that sets the stand time. That wants a sourced
table rather than three more invented numbers.
