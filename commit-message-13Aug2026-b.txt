Compute the coverage verdict instead of printing it regardless

oag_label_check --coverage closed with an unconditional print, and it told the reader to
withdraw a correct finding.

Lines 162 to 165 asserted that a normal month at the named airports means the world is
there, that the five-against-seven region count is the partitioning, and that the reading
of a coverage gap is wrong. That sentence was written for TPE, PVG and HKG, which do come
back normal. It then printed itself unchanged over DXB, DOH, JNB and CAI, where the table
directly above it shows Johannesburg at 24 distinct departing flights in 2017-08 against
402 in 2019-08.

Distinct departing flights in August as a share of each airport's own best label:

  JNB   5% / 6% / 6% in 2015-17 against 100% in 2018-19
  CAI  13% / 13% / 13%
  DOH  48% / 54% / 60%
  DXB  56% / 59% / 60%

So REGION-COVERAGE and REGION-CONSEQUENCE of 12 August stand. The store is short of
intra-Africa and intra-Middle-East schedules for 2015 to 2017, and cohorts 2016 and 2017
take every pre-launch month from that range.

THE FIX is the same one already applied to dep_basis in cortex_app: a label must say what
the code checked, not what its author expected. Each label is now scored against that
airport's own best label, which keeps the comparison inside one airport and needs no
external traffic figure. Anything below 75% is named SHORT, with the rule printed beside
it so it can be argued with, on the ground that these airports did not grow by a quarter
in a year. Where nothing falls short the script says so and prints the partitioning
reading instead.

This is the sixth instance of the recorded shape and the first where the silent default
was a conclusion rather than a value.
