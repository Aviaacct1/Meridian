Derive the control's tolerance from the stored precision, not from a flat 2%

The run stopped at 184 of 2,948 rows and the fault was the tolerance rather than the data.

backtest line 595 builds ratio_p2p from the UNROUNDED captured and p2p. Line 602 stores
captured_uncapped as an integer, and line 604 stores p2p_outturn as an integer and
fc_over_p2p to three decimals. So a ratio of two rounded integers cannot match a rounded
ratio of two unrounded ones to a flat 2%. Rounding alone moves the ratio by 2.55% where
captured is 20 and 5.05% where it is 10, and on an engine reading a median 0.617 against a
100-passenger floor that is exactly a six per cent slice of the population.

That is the TOLERANCE fault of 12 August in a new place, and the fourth of mine today. The
tolerance is now derived from the write precision: 0.5/captured + 0.5/p2p on the ratio, plus
0.0005 for the stored decimals.

VERIFIED TO DISCRIMINATE, not merely to pass. On 20,000 synthetic rows built the way the
arm's writer builds them, the derived tolerance flags zero. On 2,000 rows carrying a
genuinely 15% different numerator it still flags 1,988. The control also now prints the
first five disagreements with the rounding allowance beside each, because a bare count was
what made the first stop unreadable.

WORTH RECORDING AS PROCESS, at the end of a day with seven basis errors in it. The seats
cross-check made three, each found only after it had produced a result that was then
withdrawn. This script made one, and its own control caught it before any figure existed.
That is what a left and right hand side from one row, plus a stated-precision check, buys.

The rule earned today: build the control before the measurement, derive its tolerance from
how the values were stored, and print the exceptions rather than the count.
