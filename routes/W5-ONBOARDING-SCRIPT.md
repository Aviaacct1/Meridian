# Meridian onboarding call: the script

Draft v0.1, 21 September 2026. Written by W5 for whoever runs the first onboarding calls.
45 minutes, on video, screen shared, with the client's own routes. Commercial in confidence.

The purpose is not a tour. It is that three real routes of theirs come out of the call, that
they know what the service does not do before they find out, and that they know who to write to.
A client who runs nothing in the first fortnight will not run anything in the first year.

Before the call: the account exists and the sign-in has been tested; their catchment is loaded
if they bought it; the known-issues list current at that date is attached to the invitation;
their three routes are asked for in the invitation so they arrive with them.

## 0 to 5 minutes: who is on the call and what they want from the year

Names and what each person does. Then one question, and write the answer down: what would make
this licence worth renewing in twelve months. Everything after this is arranged around their
answer.

## 5 to 10 minutes: what it is, in their terms

One minute on the forecast: built route by route from measured demand, with its own calibrated
range on every run. The accuracy record, in the ruled words: calibrated leads are within 20% of
the outcome 89% of the time and within 10% 82% of the time, on 2,915 real launches; blind
results are reported as portfolios only, never as a single route.

One minute on what the pack is not. Say it before they find it: it is not the hand-built study
Avia would write for them, it is the same forecast with researched support around it, produced
the same day, and they can do it for every route on their list.

Then stop talking and open their first route.

## 10 to 25 minutes: their first route, run by them

They drive. Share control or let them use their own screen. Run it end to end: the route, the
forecast, the range, the schedule sizing, the economics, the pack.

What to point at as it runs, and nothing else: where the range comes from; that the frequency is
a weekly count with no day allocation yet; that the economics are indicative and why; where the
download is.

What to ask: does this match what you would have said, and if not, which side do you think is
right. Write the answer down. A gap in the first call is the most useful thing in the licence
year.

## 25 to 35 minutes: their second and third routes

Faster, with them driving. The second route shows Optimise, because the time-of-day answer is
the thing they cannot get anywhere else. The third is theirs to pick with no help, which is the
test of whether the first two landed.

If a route fails or returns something odd, say so plainly, capture it, and tell them what
happens next. Never explain it away on the call.

## 35 to 42 minutes: how the year works

- Runs, scenarios and standard packs are unlimited. Researched packs are under fair use, which
  acts as a telephone call at 250 in a year and never as a block.
- Which airports the licence covers, and that analysis works for any city pair anywhere.
- Named users on their own domain, no count, no consultant accounts.
- Support: SLOT 4, the address and who reads it, and what to expect.
- The known-issues list, and that they will be told when it changes.
- The quarterly feedback they agreed to as a launch client, and when the first one is due.

## 42 to 45 minutes: what they leave with

Say each of these out loud and then send them the same day:

1. The three routes they ran, as packs and workbooks.
2. The known-issues list as at that date.
3. The support address and the name of the person who reads it.
4. A date for the first quarterly feedback call.
5. One line on what to run next, chosen from what they said in the first five minutes.

## Afterwards, the same day

Send the five things above. Put the gaps and the questions on `MASTER-TASK-LIST.md`, not in an
inbox. Note in the lead store what they said would make the licence worth renewing, because that
sentence is what the renewal conversation is about eleven months later.
