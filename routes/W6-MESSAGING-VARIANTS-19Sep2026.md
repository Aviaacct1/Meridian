## Sentence 1: the one-liner

**1A.** Run your route on our stand and watch the forecast land in seconds: 25 years of QSI
practice, built into a tool and calibrated against real route launches.

**1B.** Bring us a route. We will run the forecast while you stand there, on the method Avia
has used by hand for 25 years, calibrated against real launches.

**1C.** Your route, forecast in seconds on the stand: the QSI method Avia has run for 25
years, now a tool, calibrated against real launches.

W6 view: 1B. It is the only one written as an instruction to the reader, and the stand needs
an instruction. 1A keeps "in seconds" in the first clause, which is the stronger claim if
John wants the claim in front.

Evidence for "in seconds": Run returned in 9.8 seconds on SJC-TPE and 9.0 seconds cold on
BRS-EWR, measured on the workstation on 19 September at commit 1012c29 (umbrella Status
block, TIMING-20260919-1937). Optimise on the same build returned in 35 to 65 seconds.
"In seconds" therefore describes Run and not Optimise. Optimise is the demonstration, and
the host introduces it as such. No copy claims seconds for Optimise.

## Sentence 2: the three sub-messages, one clause each

### 2.1 Time of day, the difference

**A.** The best time of day to fly it, not just how many will fly it.
**B.** It tells you when to fly the route, not only whether to fly it.
**C.** We forecast the departure time as well as the demand.

W6 view: A. It is the shortest and it is the one a route development manager repeats to a
colleague.

### 2.2 The pack

**A.** A researched pack with your numbers in your inbox within 30 minutes.
**B.** You leave with the run you watched, and the written pack follows within 30 minutes.
**C.** Your numbers, researched and written up, before you reach the far end of the hall.

W6 view: B. It ties the pack to the run the visitor saw, which is the one-run rule, and it
survives a slow queue better than A because it promises the run first.

### 2.3 Independent

**A.** Independent and senior: no network to sell you, no house view.
**B.** Independent since 2001, senior people only, nothing to sell you but the work.
**C.** We hold no network position, so you get the answer, not a position.

W6 view: B. It carries the 2001 date, which does the work that "senior" alone does not.

## What W6 changed from the controller's draft, and why

1. "in seconds" moved off Optimise. The measured numbers support it for Run only.
2. The offer sentence now quotes the published grid as the rulings state it, rather than a
   soft figure. The soft-figure approach in the commercial plan section 5 predates the
   published grid in the pricing note. John should say which of the two governs the stand
   answer: they are not the same position, and the host cannot hold both.
3. Nothing about any competitor appears in any variant, including by implication. "No house
   view" and "no network position" describe Avia, not anyone else.
4. Product naming throughout: Meridian, published by The Aviation Observatory. Avia Cortex
   appears nowhere.
