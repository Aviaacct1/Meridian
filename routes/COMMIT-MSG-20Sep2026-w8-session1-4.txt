band carries the whole product with unlimited runs, scenarios, exports and
researched packs under a fair-use clause, no seat count and no seat charge, named
users on the operator's corporate email domain, and no consultant ever holding a
seat. Analysis of any city pair is unrestricted on every licence; only production,

Two terms points from John, 20 September, added to section 6. Quarterly payment
is understood but not preferred for Avia's cash flow, so it carries a visible
6.7 per cent premium at clean quarterly figures of 4,000, 6,000 and 8,000, and a

The on-off pattern, signing for a year and running forecasts in advance before
taking a year off, is answered four ways: the product limits it, because a
forecast carries the schedules, demand data and calibration of the day it ran and
a pack dated eighteen months does not survive an airline's question about the
data; the launch price becomes conditional on continuous licensing, so a lapse
forfeits the remaining launch years without breaching item 29's no obligation to
renew; a lapsed licence pays 2,500 re-onboarding to return; and the honest limit
is stated rather than papered over, which is that a genuinely biennial buyer
cannot be stopped economically and has bought the wrong product, so the answer is
to put the 3,500 route study in front of them first.

Correction from John, 20 September: THERE IS NO QUARTERLY LICENCE. The licence is
always annual whether paid quarterly or annually, so the full year is due from
signature, an unpaid instalment suspends access until paid and the year remains
due as a debt. W8 had written quarterly as an access right for that quarter and
that was wrong. It also removes the front-loading worry, since a client who runs
everything in the first quarter still owes the remaining three.

John's answer to the on-off pattern is A PERMANENT DISCOUNT ON RENEWAL rather
than a minimum term, which keeps item 29's no obligation to renew intact. Three
versions costed on the 81 independent clients in the 25 per cent case: 5 per cent
from the second renewal costs 60,750 a year and pays for itself if it cuts churn
by about five points; 5 then 10 per cent costs 121,500 and needs ten points. W8
recommends 5 per cent from the second renewal, held while continuous and lost on
a lapse, with the list left at 15,000 rather than raised to 15,750, because
raising it reopens a number now in four workstreams' files four days before the
solicitor. The continuity price is Avia's best price and carries no further
discount except the group rate; the floors govern negotiated discounts only.

John's question answered in section 6: the renewal discount is off the list price
of the year, never off the price last paid, because off the price paid compounds
to 60 per cent of the original in ten years. The accurate sales sentence follows:
the price falls once at the first renewal, by 300 pounds on a single airport, and
then tracks 5 per cent below list.

Multi-year added as decision 17, split into two goods because they are not the
same. A commitment paid annually gives revenue certainty; a prepay gives
certainty plus cash and no credit risk. A three-year prepay is worth 15 to 19 per
cent to Avia on time value at an 8 per cent cost of capital plus avoided churn at
12 per cent a year, so John's 10 per cent is comfortably profitable and there is
room above it. Proposed: three years paid annually earns the 5 per cent
continuity discount from day one; two years prepaid 7.5 per cent; three years
prepaid 12.5 per cent, being 40,550 or 13,517 a year. That is also the maximum,
because it lands 17 pounds above the 13,500 floor while 15 per cent breaches it,
which gives Avia a true answer when a buyer pushes for more. A launch client may
prepay the three launch years at no further discount, which stacks nothing and is
cash upside.
