## Meridian: launch customers, year 1

Meridian is the route forecasting tool published by The Aviation Observatory. It builds a
forecast route by route from measured demand, optimises the time of day, and produces the
presentation you would otherwise commission. Calibrated leads are within 20% of the outcome 89%
of the time and within 10% 82% of the time, on 2,915 real launches; blind results are reported
as portfolios only, never as a single route.

### What you get

- The full tool. Every licence is the whole product. Price steps with the size of the airport,
  never with features held back.
- Three named seats for your route development team.
- Forecasts, scenarios and exports without limit, within fair use.
- 100 generated presentations a year across the three seats, each one the forecast you ran.
- One review call each quarter with an Avia director, on your own routes.
- The known-issues list at the start, and told when it changes. You are a launch customer, not
  a test subject.

### What you give

- Written feedback once a quarter on what you used and what failed you.
- A named reference at the end of year 1, if you are satisfied.
- Patience on hosting while we harden it: we host Meridian ourselves during year 1, with
  planned maintenance and short interruptions.

### Price and term

Published, in full, in sterling and exclusive of VAT:

| Airport size | Licence, a year |
|---|---|
| Small | £15,000 |
| Medium | £20,000 |
| Large | £25,000 |

Size is agreed in the quotation from published passenger numbers. Airlines, consultancies and
multi-airport groups are quoted on their portfolio.

Twelve months from first access, invoiced annually in advance, payable within 30 days. Year 2 is
charged at the list price then published.

SLOT A: the launch customer offer, in one sentence. John's figures: the year-1 discount, the
number of places and the date it expires. Fallback if unfilled by 3 October 2026: the sentence
comes out, and the answer on the stand and in this document stays "on request, limited places".

### What is excluded

- Presentations beyond the 100 included, which are charged for each document at the rate in the
  licence. SLOT B: the overage rate. No figure exists anywhere. It must be set before the first
  licence is issued.
- Day-of-week allocation of frequency, fare in the capture score, and the economics of the
  newest aircraft types. All three are on the published list and none is in year 1.
- Liability for decisions you take on our outputs. The aircraft economics outputs are indicative
  and built on generic published assumptions, not on any airline's actual costs.
- Data. You receive outputs, and you use them inside your organisation and with your own
  advisers. You do not redistribute or resell them.

### How it starts

Onboarding opens on 7 November 2026. It is a 45-minute call in which we run your first three
routes together, and you keep the outputs.

SLOT C: the contracting entity, the support address and the response time, as in the agreement.

## The tier question, for John

The published grid prices by airport size only (£15,000 / £20,000 / £25,000, three seats, 100
presentations) and quotes consultancies and multi-airport groups on portfolio. The commercial
plan has three tiers instead: Airport, Airline, Adviser.

W5's recommendation: use the published grid, and name airlines and advisers as quoted. Two
reasons. The grid is already printed on the staging site, so a prospect who reads the site and
then reads this document must see the same numbers or ask which is real. And no airline or
adviser price exists to print; inventing one to fill a tier is the one thing we do not do.

What that costs: an airline network planner reads a page priced by airport size and has to be
told the tier is quoted, which is a weaker answer than a number.

Fallback if John does not rule by 3 October 2026: the published grid, airlines and advisers
quoted.
