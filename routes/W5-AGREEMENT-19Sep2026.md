## 1. Parties

This agreement is made between SLOT 1 (the contracting entity, with its registered number and
registered office: Avia Solutions Limited or The Aviation Observatory Ltd. Umbrella item 31,
no default. It decides who invoices, who holds the OAG and Sabre subscriptions and who carries
the liability, so it is answered before this draft reaches the solicitor) ("the Supplier") and
the client named in the schedule ("the Client").

## 2. What the Client receives

The Supplier grants the Client a non-exclusive, non-transferable annual licence to use
Meridian, the route forecasting tool published by The Aviation Observatory, for the Client's
own airport and for the term set out in clause 3. The licence covers three named individual
users at the Client's organisation. It includes forecasts, scenarios and exports without limit
within fair use, 100 generated presentations in each year of the term across the three seats,
and SLOT 2 (the review call: the draft promises one call each quarter with an Avia director;
John confirms the cadence and that it is a director).

The Supplier names the seats on signature. LAWYER 1: seats are per named individual. The
clause needs wording on reassignment when a person leaves, and on what happens if a login is
used beyond the named seats.

## 3. Term and renewal

The term runs for twelve months from the date the Supplier gives first access, and renews
automatically for successive periods of twelve months. The Client may end the agreement at the
next renewal by giving notice at least one month before the renewal date. The Supplier sends a
renewal notice three months before each renewal date and a reminder two months before.

Where the Client declares in the schedule that its own procurement rules do not permit an
agreement that renews automatically, renewal is by the Client's written acceptance instead.
That option is available only on that declaration and carries no change in price.

LAWYER 2: the declaration wording, which is John's own flag. It has to be tight enough that
the option means something and loose enough that a public-sector buyer can sign it.
LAWYER 12: automatic renewal in a business-to-business contract, and whether the notice
periods in this clause are enough to make it enforceable.

## 4. Price: the launch terms and the three-year schedule

The Client's band is SLOT 3 (small, medium or large, determined from published passenger
numbers and agreed in the quotation. The internal thresholds that place a client in a band are
John's, by 3 October). The Client's agreement carries its own band only; all three are shown
here for the draft.

The Supplier's standard list price by band, shown for illustration over three years. The
Supplier reviews its list annually; this schedule assumes 3% a year. The contractual mechanism
is clause 5.

| Airport band | Year 1 list | Year 2 list | Year 3 list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | £15,000 | £15,450 | £15,914 |
| Medium | £20,000 | £20,600 | £21,218 |
| Large | £25,000 | £25,750 | £26,523 |

The Client is a launch client and pays, for years 1, 2 and 3, the fixed sums below. They are
50%, 75% and 85% of the list for each year in the schedule above, rounded to the nearest £50.
The illustrative list is shown to the nearest pound so that each launch figure reconciles
against it. They are stated in pounds, they are fixed at signature, and
nothing in years 1 to 3 refers to a list published later.

| Airport band | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 onward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | £7,500 | £11,600 | £13,550 | the published list |
| Medium | £10,000 | £15,450 | £18,050 | the published list |
| Large | £12,500 | £19,300 | £22,550 | the published list |

From year 4 the Client pays the Supplier's published standard price for its band at the
renewal date, with clause 5 applying. The Client is under no obligation to renew at any step.

Each year of the licence is the whole product. The price steps with the size of the airport,
never with features held back.

LAWYER 3: "the published standard price" has to be defined, or year 4 is unenforceable. The
proposed definition is the Supplier's price list for Meridian as published on its website at
the renewal date.

## 5. Escalation

The Supplier's standard prices are reviewed each year and move by at least inflation. At each
renewal the price increases by not less than the change in the UK Consumer Prices Index (Office
for National Statistics, all items, the twelve months to the preceding December), and the
Supplier may set a higher increase in the renewal notice it gives under clause 3.

The three fixed sums in clause 4 are the express exception to this clause for years 1, 2 and 3.
From year 4 the Client is on the published list and this clause applies in full.

LAWYER 4: draft the exception so that clauses 4 and 5 cannot be read against each other. If
inflation runs above 3%, the launch client's years 2 and 3 sit below the escalation floor, by
design; the drafting has to say so rather than leave it to be inferred.

## 6. Add-ons and new functions

The Supplier offers further modules and options at the prices published when they are offered.
As a launch client, the Client takes any of them at 50% of that price if it adopts in year 1,
or 75% if it adopts in year 2. Those adopted today: the Observatory Global Forecast, the Design
Day module, and SLOT 4 (the Meridian route study, the researched pack for a single route,
priced per route and invoiced on delivery with no licence. Umbrella item 35. It appears here
and on the one-pager only once John prices it).

Where the Supplier builds a function into the core product rather than offering it as an
option, the Supplier may increase the core price at the next renewal by more than clause 5
requires. It gives that increase in the renewal notice under clause 3, and the Client keeps its
right not to renew.

LAWYER 5: a clause that lets one party raise the price above the agreed mechanism invites
challenge. It needs the notice, the Client's exit and the limits stated in terms that hold.

## 7. Payment

SLOT 5 (payment terms: annual in advance, invoice on signature, payable within 14 days, with a
stated discount for payment within 14 days if John rules one. This is the controller's proposal
of 20 September and John rules it by 3 October). All prices exclude VAT and are in sterling.

Presentations generated beyond the 100 included in clause 2 are charged for each document at
SLOT 6 (the overage rate. No figure exists anywhere; the pricing note of 19 September 2026
records that it must be set before the first licence is issued. Until it is set, the 100
included is a cap and further presentations are quoted separately).

LAWYER 6: interest on late payment, and whether the Supplier may suspend access for
non-payment and after how long.

## 8. What the launch client gives

The Client receives the launch price in return for the following, which are the consideration
for it:

- It accepts that Meridian is newly launched, and works with the Supplier on the items in the
  known-issues list attached as the schedule rather than treating them as breaches.
- It gives the Supplier structured feedback on use at least once each quarter during year 1.
- It acts as a reference for the Supplier, and allows the Supplier to use its name and brand in
  the Supplier's marketing.
- It signs this agreement by 30 November 2026.

SLOT 7 (the number of launch places. John, by 3 October. It belongs in the offer rather than in
this agreement, and this clause names it only if John wants the Client to see the number it was
one of).

LAWYER 11: the use of the Client's name and brand needs its own wording: what may be used,
whether the Client approves each use, and whether it may withdraw the permission later.

## 9. The data behind the outputs

Meridian is built on the Supplier's own subscriptions to OAG and Sabre data. Both providers
have confirmed on calls, with witnesses present, that the Supplier may use the tool and share
its outputs. The Supplier is obtaining that confirmation in writing and will provide it to the
Client on request. The Client receives outputs, not data. The Client may use the outputs within
its own organisation and with its own professional advisers. The Client does not redistribute,
publish or resell them.

LAWYER 7: the clause with the most exposure. What the Supplier warrants about third-party data
rights, and what happens if a provider later restricts sharing. Clause 15 carries the
consequence; this clause carries the warranty.

## 10. What the forecast is, and what it is not

Meridian builds a forecast step by step from measured demand, and every forecast carries its
own calibrated range. On the calibration record behind it: calibrated leads are within 20% of
the outcome 89% of the time and within 10% 82% of the time, on 2,915 real launches; blind
results are reported as portfolios only, never as a single route.

The aircraft economics outputs carry the Supplier's standard disclaimer, which reads as it
reads in the tool:

> These aircraft economics outputs are indicative and intended for directional planning and
> comparison only. They are not a statement of the actual cost or profitability of any specific
> airline, aircraft or route. The figures are built from generic and published industry
> assumptions, including appraiser lease rates and values, OEM-indicative maintenance reserves,
> standardised utilisation by airline business model, and published or benchmark airport and air
> navigation charges. They do not reflect any individual operator's cabin configuration (LOPA),
> specific weight variant (MTOW), negotiated aircraft acquisition or lease terms, fuel hedging
> position, maintenance or component support agreements, crew costs, overhead structure,
> ancillary revenue or internal accounting policies. Actual results will differ, in some cases
> materially. These outputs should not be relied upon as a forecast of any particular operator's
> results, and Avia Solutions accepts no liability for any decision taken on the basis of them.

## 11. Liability

The Supplier accepts no liability for any decision the Client takes on the basis of any output
of Meridian.

LAWYER 8: the cap and the carve-outs are the solicitor's. Avia's instruction is that liability
is capped at the fees paid in the twelve months before the claim, with the usual exclusions
that cannot be limited.

## 12. Data handling

The Supplier stores no Client data. It stores the outputs Meridian generates, the routes run
and the identity of the named user who ran them, on the Supplier's own infrastructure, for
support and for improving the product. Nothing goes to a third party.

LAWYER 9: the named users are identified people, so this needs the privacy wording and a view
on whether a data processing agreement is required.

## 13. Support

The Client raises support at SLOT 8 (the support address, the person who reads it every working
day, and the response time this clause promises. John staffs it; the promise is worth nothing
without a name).

## 14. Known issues

The known-issues list current at signature is attached as the schedule to this agreement. The
Supplier gives the Client an updated list at onboarding and tells the Client when it changes
materially during the term.

## 15. The Supplier ending or suspending the service

The Supplier may end or suspend the service for any reason, including the loss or restriction
of a licence to data on which Meridian depends. It gives SLOT 9 (the notice period. The
controller's draft is three months; John rules it) months' notice where it is able to. It
refunds the unused part of the year's fee, calculated to the day, within 30 days. The Client
keeps every output already delivered and may continue to use it under clause 9. Neither party
has any further liability to the other.

LAWYER 10: John's instruction is that the solicitor drafts this clause. The data-licence trigger
is named expressly. "Where it is able to" needs wording that does not make the notice period
meaningless.

## 16. Termination

LAWYER 13: termination for material breach and for insolvency, what happens to fees already
paid, and what survives. Avia's instruction is that outputs already generated remain the
Client's to use under clause 9.

## 17. Governing law

This agreement is governed by the law of England and Wales, and the courts of England and
Wales have exclusive jurisdiction.
