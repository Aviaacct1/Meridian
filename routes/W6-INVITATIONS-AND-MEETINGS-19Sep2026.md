# The five meeting invitations, and the meetings logistics

Author: Avia Solutions. Version 3, 20 September 2026. Rewritten to John's meetings ruling of
20 September, 19:30: every invitation asks the airport to NAME A ROUTE it wants to learn more
about, and The Aviation Observatory forecasts it ahead of the meeting. W6's proposed pre-run
route per airport is demoted to an example, offered only if they do not name one.

Drafts in John's voice for his approval. John sends them, 26 to 29 September, through the
Routes meeting system or by email. W6 sends nothing.

The five are John's buyer-test list, which he calls "a start": Birmingham, Dublin (daa),
Vienna, Dallas Fort Worth, Milan SEA. Dublin and SEA are groups, which is the group head
office the plan wanted on the list. More may be added.

## What changed, and why it is better

The old draft named a route at each airport from what W6 could find of their public pitching
activity. Three of the five rested on a published statement, one rested on nothing, and all
five risked opening with a route the airport had already dismissed. Asking them to name it
removes the guess, makes the meeting theirs before it starts, and turns the invitation itself
into a qualifying question: an airport that names a route has a live case and a person who
owns it. The example stays in the paragraph as a fallback, so nobody has to think of one to
reply.

**One consequence to hold.** A named route arrives days before the meeting, so the pre-run
must be quick and cannot wait on a person: W1's pre-warm covers the five airports, and whoever
receives the reply forwards the route to the stand build the same day. If a route lands with
less than 48 hours to go, it runs live in the meeting instead, which the tool can do.

## Meetings logistics

**Scheduling.** Five meetings across 21 to 23 October, stand F124, Frankfurt. W6 proposes
holding Tuesday 21 and Wednesday 22 for the five and leaving Thursday 23 for walk-ups and the
reserve list, because the hall thins on the last day and a meeting that slips has somewhere to
go. Requests go 26 to 29 September. Tampa exhibits and is a client, so a stand visit covers it
without a slot.

| Pack printed, one per attendee plus two | W3 (PDF render) | 16 Oct | PDF render due 8 Oct |
| Optimise live on the meeting route | W1 and W2 | 19 Oct | Optimise at 40 to 55 seconds on the narrowed default |
| One-pager left behind, carrying the offer in writing | W5 owns it, John approves | 16 Oct | Quotes `PRICING-DECISION-2026.md` v1.0: year 1 at 50% of list, years 2 and 3 fixed in cash at signature, signing closes 30 November, no limit on places |
| Lead written to the store the same day | W5 | 21 Oct | Leads table is W2 and W5's build item |

**Pack list to carry, per meeting:** the printed pack for their route, John's card with the
site URL on it, and the one-pager. The host's spoken price answer is the sentence in
`PRICING-DECISION-2026.md` v1.0 section 7, quoted and not paraphrased, and it follows the
qualifying question "is route development done here, or at group?", because the answer decides
which price applies.

**Order-ready is 21 October, not 7 November.** John does not expect a signature at Routes, but
Avia must not be the reason a client waits: the agreement, the one-pager, the known-issues list
and the onboarding steps all exist by the 21st, so an airport with budget this year can sign
straight after the show and be running in November.

**After each meeting:** the pack goes to every attendee by email from the Observatory sender
the same day, the lead is typed into the store that evening, and the follow-up is booked inside
48 hours.
