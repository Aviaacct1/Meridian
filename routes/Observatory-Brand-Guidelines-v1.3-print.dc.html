THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
The mark represents measurement. The type represents scholarship. Together they become authority.

Every decision in this document serves that sentence. When a choice is unclear, return to it: does this make the work read as measured, scholarly, and therefore authoritative? If not, it is wrong for The Aviation Observatory.

01 · FOUNDATION

An institution, not a start-up

The Aviation Observatory publishes independent forecasts and the residual between forecast and outcome. The identity must read as established — closer to a national observatory or a learned society than to a software company. It should feel as though it could have existed for a century and will hold for another.

This is achieved through restraint. A small, deliberate set of marks; two typefaces doing precise jobs; a palette that behaves like instrument brass and printed paper rather than a screen. The system is built to survive expansion beyond aviation — to shipping, energy, or any domain that can be measured — without redrawing a single element.

MEASURED
Every graphic is an instrument reading. Nothing is decorative for its own sake.
SCHOLARLY
We show the working. Sources, methods and residuals are printed, not hidden.
ENDURING
The system reads correctly in 1890 engraving and 2050 interface alike.

03 · MARK CONSTRUCTION

The device, explained

AThe graticule. Tilted rings — the armillary sphere. This is the instrument of measurement, not a map. It carries no country outlines and is never filled.
BThe great circle. One brass arc, asymmetric and offset. It is the observed line — the single reading the Observatory reports. Never symmetric (the “sad face”).
CThe point. The observation itself, at centre, clear of the arc.
CLEAR SPACE

Keep clear space of one meridian-radius (the device’s inner ring radius) on all sides of any mark. Nothing intrudes into that field.

MINIMUM SIZE

Device: 16 px on screen, 10 mm in print. Wordmark: 90 px / 24 mm. Below these the great circle closes up.

MISUSE
Don’t fill the globe or add country outlines — it is an instrument, not a map.
Don’t flatten the great circle into a symmetric arc.
Don’t recolour, gradient or add a second accent to the brass.
Don’t lock the three marks together into one crest.
Don’t place the device on a busy image without its clear-space field.

07 · VOICE

Show the working

The Aviation Observatory speaks with understated authority. It states what it measured, how, and where it was wrong — and lets the rigour carry the confidence. It never oversells.

WE SOUND LIKE
“Our 2024 projection is printed alongside, unretouched.”

“We publish the residual — the distance between forecast and outcome.”

“Consensus is the unweighted mean of four published forecasts. Method in Appendix C.”
WE NEVER SOUND LIKE
“Revolutionary AI-powered insights!”

“The only forecast you’ll ever need.”

“Game-changing, next-generation aviation intelligence.”

“Scholarship, in this house, means showing the working.”

The Aviation Observatory's philosophy is a chart: forecast, outcome, and the residual between them. That chart is the house signature. Every element below is a fixed rule — reproduced identically wherever the Observatory plots a forecast against the record.

A · THE OBSERVED LINE
Outcome is drawn in brass at 2.4 px, rounded — the single heaviest stroke on the plate. It is always the thing that actually happened.
B · THE OPINION
Forecast is ink, hairline, dashed — visibly a prediction, deliberately lighter than the record it is judged against.
C · THE RESIDUAL
The gap between the two is filled in brass at 16% opacity — never a third line. The residual is a shape you can see, not a number you must find.
D · REGISTRATION TICKS
Four L-shaped corner marks frame the plot like an engraving plate or an instrument dial — the most ownable, unrepeatable detail.
E · DIRECT LABELS
Series are named at the end of the line in italic Newsreader. the Observatory never prints a floating legend key.
F · THE PROVENANCE RAIL
A ruled metadata footer, Inter tracked, carries source, date, units and method on every figure — the visible “working.”

13 · PHOTOGRAPHY

Photography, on a leash

Colour photography — not duotone — is what lifts this brand: an aircraft wing at golden hour, a network of city lights at night, brass instruments in low light. Geometry cannot supply scale, atmosphere or weather. The one rule is that the colour must already live in the brand's palette: deep Observatory Ink shadows, warm brass highlights, everything else desaturated. The image obeys the identity — it is never allowed to fight it.

14 · BESPOKE IMAGERY

Mood is ours. Evidence is theirs.

A commissioned report often needs photographs the library cannot hold: the client's airport, its single runway, the port on its doorstep, the city it serves. Those images are not a breach of this system. They enter it as a separate class, because imagery in an Observatory document does two different jobs and only one of them has to come from the library.

Mood opens the document. It carries the cover, the section dividers and any full-bleed frame, and it must be Observatory photography, because that is where the brand is recognised. Evidence sits in the body and supports a claim already made on the page. It may be bespoke, and it is governed as a plate — framed, graded, captioned and credited, sized like a data table rather than a hero.

MOOD · LIBRARY ONLY
Cover · section dividers · full-bleed · closing page. Graded library frames beneath a multiply toward Observatory Ink, brass hairline at the seam. Never a client photograph, never stock.
EVIDENCE · BESPOKE PERMITTED
Body grid only, inside the measure of the text. One plate per claim. Framed, graded, captioned with subject, source and date. A plate that illustrates nothing is cut.
THE FIVE RULES
01
One grade, applied to everything
Observatory Ink in the shadows, warm brass in the light, low saturation. The recipe is set out on the following page, and it is not negotiable per image. Run any third-party photograph through it and it joins the family.
02
One frame
A single brass hairline, square corners, no shadow, no rounding, no inset border. A mono caption slug beneath carrying subject, source and date. Eight photographers still read as one document.
03
Fixed placements
Body grid only. Never full-bleed, never behind type, never on a divider, cover or closing page — those positions stay library-only. At most one plate to a page or slide, four to a report.
04
A stated fallback
Where no usable, rights-cleared photograph exists, the slot takes a mono-ruled map or diagram in the same frame. Never a stock substitute. This is the data rule — flag rather than fill — extended to pictures.
05
Every plate earns its place
A plate is an argument, not decoration. It must support a claim already written on the page, and the caption must say which. If the claim would stand alone, the plate goes.

APPENDIX B

Why “Aviation” is load-bearing

The institution is The Aviation Observatory. The sector word is not decoration and it is not optional — it is the element that separates us from an existing US firm, The Observatory Group, which publishes macro market views to institutional clients under prior use. Two further neighbours sit close: The Observer, which a serif word set alone can echo as a newspaper masthead. Both risks are answered by the same discipline: carry “Aviation” at full weight, never present the name as a masthead, and let the instrument do the differentiating.

DO
Set “Aviation” at the same size as “Observatory” — equal weight, never a whisper above it.
Pair the wordmark with the device or descriptor in first-encounter use.
Hold the instrument register — armillary, brass, ink; measurement, not news.
NEVER (MASTHEAD CONVENTIONS)
Never drop “Aviation” in first-encounter, cover, contractual or trademark use.
Never set “Aviation” smaller than the word it qualifies.
No rules above & below a centred word (the newspaper banner); no dateline or edition line.
Don’t let the editorial voice tip into “press.”

This appendix fixes the tiers so the portfolio stays legible: what is an institution, what is a product, what is a module, and what stays purely internal. The governing rule — a thing earns a market name only when a client buys it by that name. Everything else is described functionally or kept internal.

TIER 1
Institution
The endorsing name. One only. Appears in chrome, footers, and on every product lockup.

THE PEER SET · WHY THE FORECAST ABBREVIATES

The reference forecasts this product is measured against are all known by a three-letter abbreviation, and buyers say those letters aloud in meetings. An unabbreviated name is a disadvantage in that company. Observatory Global Forecast is therefore built to abbreviate: OGF — and the leading O carries the institution, so the short form endorses itself.

HOW TO USE IT
Spell the name in full on first mention, then abbreviate: “the Observatory Global Forecast (OGF)…”
Set OGF in the same figure style as the peer abbreviations — no periods, no spacing, never O.G.F.
The tool and the annual share the name; the edition year separates them — OGF 2026. The annual is titled Observatory Global Forecast with the edition year, not “Outlook” — one name, two deliverables.
In charts and comparison tables, OGF may stand alone beside CMO and GMF.
NEVER
Never let OGF replace the institution in a lockup — the abbreviation is a reference convention, not a mark.
Never abbreviate a Tier 3 module (no DDFS in client-facing copy).
Never introduce a second abbreviation for the same product.
THE RULES
Engines and methodologies stay internal — they appear only in provenance and method notes, never as a brand.
Modules are described functionally and endorsed by their product until they earn a tier.
A product name triggers a full lockup (App. B) and a trademark check before it hardens into UI, docs, and exports.
Internally, use plain descriptive working names — never a half-formed brand a client might see.
WHEN A MODULE BECOMES A PRODUCT
Promote a module to Tier 2 only when a client can buy it on its own, it is priced separately, and it is sold under its own name. Until all three are true, keep it functional — e.g. “Observatory Global Forecast — Design Day module.” Under-naming is free and reversible; over-naming costs confused clients and wasted clearance.
STANDING GUIDANCE
Name late, not early. The portfolio should read as one institution with a few clear products — not a shelf of competing brands. Every new name must justify a tier before it is spent.
