## 3. What the stand looks like to a passer-by

Three walls, open to the aisle; the back wall faces the aisle and is the hero. The default
plan puts the counter at the front centre, the high table with four stools in the middle,
the TV on a floor stand in the back right corner, angled, and the bin back left. From the
aisle, therefore:

- The counter (circa 945mm tall) hides the middle of the back wall below that height.
- The table group and anyone on a stool hide the centre of the back wall up to standing head
  height. Working assumption: circa 1,700mm, until W3 checks it against the render.
- The TV on its stand covers part of the back right of the back wall and the right wall.
- The band that stays visible from the aisle is circa 1,750mm to 2,500mm on the back wall,
  and the aisle ends of both side walls at the same height.

## 4. The design rule: stop, then read, then ask

1. **The stopper, high.** In the band 1,750mm to 2,500mm on the back wall: the call to action
   (section 5), the largest type on the stand, readable from across the aisle at walking pace.
2. **The name, high.** The product lockup (The Aviation Observatory / Meridian / ROUTE
   FORECASTING, three lines, Appendix B) in the same band, left of the stopper, clear of the TV.
3. **The second line, just under it**, where the table group does not reach.
4. **Below circa 1,700mm, nothing that must be read.** Ink ground, the corner registration
   ticks at the wall edges.
5. **Side walls are read at an angle.** Their aisle ends, high, carry the site address and the
   stacked lockup with descriptor (The Aviation Observatory / FORECASTS & THE RESIDUAL).
6. **No word crosses a dashed line on the plan** until Full Vision confirms what they are.

Furniture: if it can move, W9 proposes the counter to the front left corner on the aisle edge
(the welcome and the email capture), the table group to the back left, and the TV left where
it is, back right. That clears the centre and right of the back wall for the stopper. If it
cannot move, the stopper sits on the upper left two-thirds of the back wall, above the table.

## 5. The call to action (John, 25 Sep: D, without sounding like a crystal ball)

**No Avia Solutions anywhere on the stand** (John, 25 Sep). The stand is The Aviation
Observatory and Meridian only.

The claims the programme may make (W6-RULINGS, 21 Sep 16:30 and 26 Sep sweep): the FORECAST
"in about a minute" for a single run; "under five minutes" for the whole demonstration;
Optimise "a few minutes more", never in seconds.

John prefers D, "Any city pair. Forecast in about a minute." The risk he names is the
fortune-teller register. W9's reading of where that risk sits, and the answer:

1. **"Any" is the overclaim.** It promises every pair works, and pre-mortem 2 says thin
   markets will not. The visitor's own pair is the stronger stopper and the honest one.
2. **Speed alone reads as a trick.** A time with nothing beside it invites "how, then?". A
   second line that shows the method answers it before it is asked, which is the brand's own
   rule ("show the working").
3. **The design carries half of it.** No stopwatch, timer, countdown or "60 seconds"; no
   exclamation marks; "forecast", never "predict"; Newsreader on Ink, the registration ticks
   framing the wall like an instrument plate. Understated type is what separates a measured
   claim from a fairground one.

| | Top band (stopper) | Second line, under it |
|---|---|---|
| D1, adopted by John 25 Sep (refine in the design session if need be) | Your city pair. Forecast in about a minute. | Built from airline schedules and fares, with the method shown. |
| D2 | Name a city pair. See its forecast in about a minute. | Every forecast shows its working. |
| D, as John wrote it | Any city pair. Forecast in about a minute. | Every forecast shows its working. |

The second lines must be true of the product on the stand before they are printed: W6 to
confirm against the screen W2 builds (sources and method visible on the run). If neither can
be confirmed, the second line is sentence 2.1, "The optimal time to fly it, not just how many
will fly it", which is true today and carries Optimise. W6 owns the words and hands them to W3
on 27 Sep; this is W9's input with John's steer.

## 7. Brand rules that bind the wall (from the guidelines, v1.3)

- **Colour.** Ink #0F1B28, Brass #D4A249, Paper #F6F3EC, Instrument Grey #C4CDD6. Brass is
  the only accent, spent once; never tinted, never a gradient. Paper, not pure white.
- **Type.** Newsreader for the statements; Inter, tracked, for labels and the address; Plex
  Mono only for genuine machine output, so not on the wall.
- **Marks.** Wordmark for identification; device only small (counter); seal not on the stand.
  Clear space one inner-ring radius; wordmark minimum 24mm, device 10mm (no risk at stand
  size). Marks never locked together into a crest.
- **Charts.** Every chart carries its provenance rail (source, date, units, scenario, method)
  and the brass line is always the observed reading. A chart without numbers breaks the first
  rule and the accuracy figure is ruled off the wall (25 Sep late), so **no chart on the
  wall**. The residual plot belongs on the screen and in the pack.
- **Photography: none on the walls.** Full Vision requires 300dpi minimum. The back wall at
  3,960mm is circa 46,800 pixels wide at 300dpi; the library frames on file are circa 1,536
  pixels. The walls are built as vector artwork (type, colour, linework, the marks), which
  has no resolution limit, with text outlined as Full Vision asks.
- **Voice.** Understated. No "revolutionary", no "AI-powered", no "game-changing". Nothing
  about any competitor (programme rule). The guidelines' naming appendix names other firms;
  none of that goes near the stand.

## 8. The print gap the guidelines leave

The guidelines give colour in screen values (hex and RGB) only; there are no CMYK builds and
no Pantone references. Large-format print is CMYK, and Ink and Brass are the two colours most
likely to shift. Full Vision's specification states no colour profile; W3 asks for it (or sets FOGRA39 as a working assumption, marked to confirm), converts, and records the CMYK values used;
W9 asks FV for a printed colour proof or a swatch strip before the full run if their timeline
allows. John to decide whether the CMYK builds, once set, go into the guidelines as v1.4.
