The engine reconciles to the 2025 analyst leg by leg, and the growth taper was the whole gap

No engine code. Two guards on econ_baseline.py found by running it, and the record of an evening
that produced the reconciliation this project has claimed since Monday and never demonstrated.

1. THE RECONCILIATION, AND WHY THE OLD ONE WAS NOT ONE

The "1.03x the analyst on total, 1.01x on P2P" quoted in the handover, in commit-message-10 and in
verify_connecting_build section 11 compares Meridian's CAPPED CARRIED figure against the analyst's
UNCAPPED DEMAND. They agree because our plan cap of 87.5% sits near his DERIVED 86.4% and the
aircraft are similar sizes. That is arithmetic on seats agreeing with arithmetic on seats. His
86.4% was an output of his forecast; our 87.5% is an input to ours. WITHDRAWN.

Read uncapped on both sides, on his 12:00 schedule, his A359 at 306 seats, Southwest counted as a
partner as his scope states, floor off so the connecting leg is comparable with his, two-way:

  2027, post-recovery 7/7      P2P  82,196  vs 81,858 = 1.004x
                               cnx  27,570  vs 25,999 = 1.060x
                               tot 109,764  vs 107,857 = 1.018x
  2028, post-recovery 7/7/7    P2P 1.074x   cnx 1.135x   tot 1.089x
  2028, analyst 16/7/7         P2P 1.165x   cnx 1.230x   tot 1.181x

AND THE MIX AGREES. His connecting share is 24.1%. Ours reads 25.1% at 4x, 24.3% at 5x, 23.8% at 6x.

2. THE GAP WAS THE GROWTH TAPER, NOT THE MARKET BUILD

On the default taper the same case reads 154,466 two-way, 1.43x the analyst. The taper measures a
20.00% market CAGR, which is the CLAMP CEILING and a post-COVID recovery burst, holds it for two
years and decays to 3%, giving +64.6% to 2028. The analyst built +16% / +7% / +7% = +32.8%.
1.646 / 1.328 = 1.239, so 25 of the 43 points are that one assumption.

DECIDED: the recovery is over and the default taper is not appropriate for client work. Scenarios
carry an explicit post-recovery growth path. The taper should not be a silent default on a route
that has not launched.

3. THE FREQUENCY FINDING, WHICH IS WHAT AN AIRLINE ACTUALLY BUYS

Two-way demand against two-way seats, post-recovery growth:

  2027  4x   109,764 / 127,296 = 86.2%
  2027  5x   119,306 / 159,120 = 75.0%
  2028  4x   117,448 / 127,296 = 92.3%
  2028  5x   127,658 / 159,120 = 80.2%
  2028  6x   137,200 / 190,944 = 71.9%

The analyst's 4x is validated for launch. The route outgrows it: at 2028 it spills circa 6,064
two-way, 5% of demand, and the fifth frequency clears that at 80.2%. Six weekly is too thin.

4. AND THE FLOOR IS NOW EVIDENCED, WHICH CHANGES WHAT AGREEMENT MEANS

Actual connecting per route is outturn_pax less p2p_outturn. The multiplier the flat feed needs to
reach it: all routes x0.94 (n=2,339), hub destination x1.03 (n=1,510), long haul over 6,000km
x2.50 (n=476), touching a major Asian hub x2.07 (n=335), long haul AND Asian hub x2.82 (n=147).
On SJC-TPE, 10,440km into Taipei, split_share applies x2.19.

THE FLOOR IS SIZED CORRECTLY FOR THE CASE IT WAS WRITTEN FOR, and it is conservative against the
closest comparator. Its design is right too: it only ever LIFTS, and where the feed already reads
0.94 to 1.03 it has nothing to lift.

The uncomfortable consequence: our raw feed agrees with the analyst at circa 25% connecting because
BOTH ARE BUILT ON THE SAME UNDER-COUNTING SOURCE. Agreeing with him on the mix is not validation.

5. TWO GUARDS ON econ_baseline.py, BOTH FOUND BY RUNNING IT

The first capture ran in a fresh shell with AVIA_OAG and AVIA_SABRE unset. All three cases returned
"OAG/Sabre databases not found" and the file was written anyway, with those strings as the frozen
figures. A later check would have compared error against error and reported that nothing moved.
capture now refuses to write when any case fails, and names each one.

It would also have frozen the wrong basis. AVIA_FREQ_SENSITIVE was decided ON on 10 August; with it
off the capture share reads 0.32 rather than 0.2513 and the route returns the same demand at every
frequency. capture now refuses to write without it. The check side already blocked on a switch
difference; the capture side did not.

datetime.utcnow() is deprecated in python 3.12 and printed a warning across the first line of
output. Replaced with the timezone-aware form.

6. TWO THINGS OPENED, NEITHER RESOLVED

THE DEPARTURE OPTIMISER SCORES CONNECTING PASSENGERS ONLY. route_feed.optimise_departure returns
b_pax + h_pax and picks the maximum; local demand and yield are not in the objective. On SJC-TPE it
chooses 00:30, taking P2P from 83,408 to 55,306 while taking connecting from 31,982 to 96,680.
AGAINST THAT: it picks 00:30 CI, 02:00 BR and 00:30 UA against actual SFO departures of 01:05,
01:15 and 23:55, three carriers within 35 minutes. On a 13-hour sector local demand is close to
time-indifferent and the connecting bank is not, so the objective may be capturing the binding
constraint. Test by weighting connecting at 0.7 of a local passenger and seeing whether the answer
moves. Airline-specific prorates are not obtainable and are not needed for that test.

THE 87.5% PLAN CAP IS ONE GLOBAL NUMBER for every carrier type and haul, and it can only ever
reduce carried, so it can only ever under-forecast. ULCCs routinely exceed 90% and some full-service
long-haul runs in the sixties. The attempt to set it from the back-test FAILED and its numbers must
not be used: capacity is annualised at 52 weeks in both directions from a schedule snapshot while
outturn_pax is what actually flew, so the ratio is not a load factor. Carry months-operated into the
back-test row and re-cut.

7. FOUR OF MY OWN CONCLUSIONS WITHDRAWN IN ONE DAY

Capped carried against uncapped demand, twice. The analyst's connecting figure treated as a target
when it inherits the same under-count our raw feed does. And annualised capacity against actual
outturn. Identical pattern in all four: reaching for a measurement without first establishing that
the two quantities were comparable. The three findings that survived all came from checking the
basis before reading the number.
