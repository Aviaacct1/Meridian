The departure slider: traffic at any time on the curve, derived not interpolated

JOHN, 14 August: "I was only thinking about the traffic load factor connecting numbers not
the economics... to see the forecast at the time you select with the slider without an
extra run."

IT CAN BE EXACT, WHICH IS WHY IT IS WORTH DOING. The curve is market x QSI share BEFORE the
feed level k is applied, so the ratio between the feed the run reported and the curve value
at the departure the run used is a scale that reproduces the feed at any other point,
without needing k, the growth factor or the alliance weighting. Local demand does not vary
with departure time anywhere in the engine, which WEIGHT-IS-A-NULL-TEST established, so
total demand is local plus the scaled feed, the plan cap applies as route_forecast applies
it, and the load factor follows.

Checked against the live SJC-TPE run: at the run's own 20:59 the slider returns connecting
45,138, which is exactly twice the reported feed, and a total of 81,996, which is the figure
on the page. At 19:00 it returns 72,968 and at 10:00 59,427, and the cap binds at 83,538
two-way.

app/cortex_app.py
  capacity.plan_cap added. capacity.load is the ACHIEVED load factor and equals the cap only
  when the run is capacity bound, so the two are not interchangeable and the slider needs the
  cap itself to apply the same ceiling the engine does.

app/cortex_dashboard.html
  A slider under the curve. It snaps to the nearest SCORED point rather than interpolating
  between two, so every figure shown is one the optimiser actually computed. It reports
  connecting, total demand, carried, load factor and spill, all two-way.

  WHAT IT DELIBERATELY WILL NOT SHOW, and the readout says so: the point to point and
  connecting split of what is carried, and every economic figure. Neither is linear in the
  feed, both need the engine, and an interpolated P&L on screen would be an invented number.

  The slider stays hidden rather than guessing when the run carries no feed figure or the
  curve has no point at the departure used, since there is then nothing to anchor the scale
  on.
