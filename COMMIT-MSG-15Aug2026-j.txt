Jobs 2 and 3's remainder. Everything here is presentation fed from the payload; no

app/forecast_to_contract.py
  _fill_forecast_table(): the three columns slide 32 has and the contract did not.
  deck_contract line 454 sets demand_at_service_year to None with the note "no
  pre-stimulation local leg in the payload", which was true of the block it reads and
  not of the payload: demand.stimulation is reported, so the pre-stimulation figure is
  the post-stimulation one divided by it. Arithmetic on two figures in one payload, not
  a new estimate. Also stimulation_factor, 1.0 on the connecting legs because the engine
  does not stimulate them and a blank invites the reader to assume it does, and
  annual_growth_rate as TOTAL growth base year to service year, which is the column the
  2025 deck carries.

  _fill_competition(): the competed and uncompeted rows, from cortex_app's own
  competition_split. The rates are MEASURED, one capture per market applied by the engine
  and reported by bucket, and the basis note says so, because a reader who has seen the
  2025 analyst's table will otherwise take them for two rates assumed in advance. They
  are not: COMPETITION-SPLIT-IS-NOT-THE-GAP records why building his two-rate model would
  make the forecast worse, not better.

  _settings: curfew_cost and feed_level are now SENTENCES. forecast_pack prints both
  straight into prose, and the version I wrote this morning put dicts there, which threw
  inside python-pptx on the first real render. The structured figures are kept beside
  them as curfew_cost_detail and feed_level_detail, so nothing is lost and neither
  consumer has to guess a shape. base_year added, which the table headings need.

deck/forecast_pack.py
  Disclaimer names THE AVIATION OBSERVATORY and Meridian, not Avia Solutions, and names
  the party it was prepared for. This is not an Avia Solutions product.
  "Load factor", not "Planned load factor": the figure is what the forecast produces and
  calling it planned invites the reader to take it for an input.
  Connecting tables carry the three-letter code BEFORE the city name, and the base demand
  and forecast headings carry their years.
  Traffic forecast table rebuilt to slide 32's column order: base annual demand with its
  year, traffic growth, demand before stimulation at the service year, stimulation,
  capture rate, forecast traffic with its year, per trip each way. The competed and
  uncompeted rows sit indented under their own leg. Six notes underneath.
  IMAGES: the pack rendered with no resolver, so every slot resolved to nothing and the
  cover came out blank while the Observatory path passes avia_slots.SlotResolver. Same
  resolver here, with the origin airport's coordinates so the cover globe is centred on
  the departure city. A resolver that cannot be built is not fatal and is reported.
  Two prose fields are coerced to str: the renderer takes a cryptic TypeError deep inside
  python-pptx when a non-string reaches a run, and a contract field that changes shape
  must not be able to bring the render down.

app/contract_legs_check.py
  A fifth invariant. The bucket rows sit UNDER their leg in the table, so a reader adds
  them up: their forecasts must sum to the leg and their bases must not exceed the leg's
  base. The bucket bases come from the feed DETAIL and the leg base from the market total,
  which is precisely the pair of quantities that diverged on 14 August. Tested both ways:
  a fixture whose hub buckets sum to 729,702 against a leg base of 696,196 fails, the
  corrected one passes.

slot rather than producing it blank in silence.
