Grading basis questions 1 and 2 answered: p2p_carried is not launch_pax

The document said that if they differ, option A carries the same fault as option B and the
choice collapses. It differs.

FOUR TRANSFORMATIONS, and a client's P2P number passes through all four: captured, the
uncapped local demand leg; the 87.5% plan cap at route_forecast line 760, which caps P2P and
connecting together and then pro-rates P2P by carried/total_demand at line 772; the
connectivity floor at lines 790 to 803; and the payload figure. launch_pax passes through
none of them, being a count of passengers whose whole journey was the nonstop, at whatever
load factor the airline achieved.

ON THE FROZEN CASE, SJC-TPE CI A359 306 seats 4x 2028, two-way, every figure already in the
log and the arithmetic between them reconciling to the passenger:

  captured, uncapped local demand      87,948
  after the 87.5% plan cap pro-rate    83,408    x0.9484
  after the connectivity floor         50,068    x0.6003
  what the client is shown             50,068

So the number a claim measured on launch_pax would describe and the number on the page
differ by a factor of 1.757.

AND THE CAP COUPLES THE LEGS. It pro-rates the local leg because connecting demand competed
for the same seats, so a route whose feed is over-large reports a smaller P2P figure with no
change in the local market at all. RECUT-RESULT measured that feed over-reading by circa ten
times, so the feed level reaches the local number as well as the connecting one.

QUESTION 2. launch_pax is sum(passengers) where itinerary='NON-STOP' and source_year=L,
grouped on least/greatest so both directions are summed and not averaged, over the full
calendar year rather than the months operated, with no cabin, point-of-sale or carrier
condition. The carrier recorded beside it is picked separately as the largest
operating_airline by passengers, so it names the busiest operator and not necessarily the
launching one.

FOUND WHILE ANSWERING IT, and not previously recorded: launch_pax is filtered to NON-STOP
while base_mkt at line 76 has no itinerary filter at all. The MINBASE and MAXRATIO
exclusions therefore compare a nonstop-only numerator against an all-itineraries
denominator, so the 2,000 floor is a lower bar than it reads and the 5x ceiling bites less
often than a like-for-like ratio would. Neither is wrong provided it is stated, and it is
stated nowhere. It bears on Task 3.

WHAT IS LEFT TO DECIDE is narrower than v3.0 framed it and it is commercial: re-grade on the
client's number, publish the claim on a named intermediate, or change what the page shows so
the claim and the number are the same quantity. The answer document sets out all three. I
would not start the wiring until one is chosen, because the wiring is where the claim
attaches.
