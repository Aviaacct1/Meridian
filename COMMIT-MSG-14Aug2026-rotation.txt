The slack belongs at the base: the return lands before the next departure

JOHN, 14 August, on a draw that put 16h45 on the ground at San Jose: "This still has the
18 hour layover in SJC which is not sensible."

He is right and the search I wrote an hour earlier was backwards. It stepped FORWARD from
the earliest possible turn, so it found the earliest legal return departure and therefore
the LONGEST possible sit at the origin. On SJC-TPE with a 22:59 outbound it drew the
return arriving 06:14 against a next departure at 22:59.

WHY THE TWO ENDS ARE NOT THE SAME. The aircraft does not shuttle, which John established
earlier: it flies another route from the destination and comes back. The destination end
can therefore carry a long gap, because the aircraft is earning through it. The origin end
cannot. A route station has no maintenance, no crew base and parking to pay for, so an
aircraft sitting there is idle cost and no planner would fly it.

app/cortex_app.py
  _schedule_times now times the return to LAND a short turn before the next outbound, not
  to leave as early as it can. The target is dep_out minus ground_h, default three hours,
  and the search steps BACKWARD in quarter-hours from it, so the first legal time found is
  the one that keeps the origin ground time as short as the two curfews allow. There is no
  minimum turn at the destination, because the returning aircraft is not the one that
  arrived. ground_origin_h is returned so the result is stated rather than inferred from
  two clock times.

app/cortex_dashboard.html
  The schedule line carries the ground time at the origin. It is the number that showed the
  draw was wrong, so it belongs on the page rather than in the payload.
