The workbook writer expected three keys the builder never writes

emit_workbook failed three times in succession on a live contract, each on a different key,
because it had only ever run against ba_lhr_sjc_reference(), which fills every row by hand.

  base_annual_demand   on two of the four summary rows
  nr                   on the destination cities, which build_contract passes through exactly
                       as given while wrapping the hub cities itself
  annual_demand        on the destination TOTAL, which build_contract writes with
                       annual_forecast and pdew alone

All three are the writer expecting what the builder does not produce, in one file, and none is
a model failure. Each is fixed with .get() rather than a new required key, because in every
case the figure genuinely does not exist: the connecting build starts at the market rather
than at a base that grew into it, so a blank cell states that and a zero would not.

AFTER THE THIRD ROUND TRIP I STOPPED GUESSING ONE KEY AT A TIME and checked every hard
subscript in emit_workbook against what build_contract writes, in one pass. Only three came
back unmatched, r["segment"], r["annual_growth_rate"] and r["stimulation_factor"], and all
three are written by segment_model rather than by build_contract, so they are correct.
